In this blog post, we'll explore how to effectively utilize [[Miss Neura]], a [[👩🏾‍🏫 Miss Neura (Scholar)]] tool, to fill your Obsidian vault with well-structured, informative content. This will not only satisfy your curiosity but also leverage the power of the large language model integrated into your system for efficient querying.

## Step 1: Setting Up Your Research Framework

Before diving into the research, it's crucial to set up a framework that is optimized for Obsidian. This involves:

- **Markdown Formatting**: Use Markdown to create documents that are easily transferable to Obsidian.
- **Wikilinks**: Implement square brackets `[[ ]]` to create backlinks for keywords, concepts, people, events, etc.
- **Tags**: Utilize tags to categorize your content and enhance searchability within your vault.

## Step 2: Crafting the Research Prompt

Miss Neura requires a clear and concise prompt to begin the research process. Here's an example prompt you can use or modify:

```markdown
Research Topic: Japanese Myths
- Create an outline to ensure the topic aligns with your interests.
- Gather information from reputable sources.
- Structure the content with a blog name, subsections, and comprehensive details.
- Include a critique section for a balanced view.
- List citations and tags at the end of the document.
```

## Step 3: Conducting the Research

With the prompt ready, Miss Neura will search the web for relevant information. It will then present an outline for your approval. Once confirmed, Miss Neura will compile the research into a Markdown document.

## Step 4: Creating the Markdown Article

Miss Neura will provide the researched content in a code block, making it easy to copy and paste directly into your Obsidian vault. Here's an example of what the Markdown might look like:

```markdown
# Japanese Mythology

## Overview
Content about the general aspects of Japanese mythology.

## Major Texts
- [[Kojiki]]
- [[Nihon Shoki]]
- [[Shintoshu]]

## Critique
A section providing different perspectives on Japanese mythology.

## Citations
1. Source 1
2. Source 2

## Tags
- #JapaneseMythology
- #Folklore
- #Myths
```

## Step 5: Expanding Your Knowledge

Once the initial article is in your vault, you can delve deeper into specific areas of interest. For example, if you want to learn more about the "Kojiki," you can ask Miss Neura to research this topic next. Repeat the process to create a comprehensive knowledge base.

## Step 6: Following Your Curiosity

As you continue to explore topics, Miss Neura will assist you in creating interconnected articles. For instance, if you're interested in "The Legend of Amaterasu," simply prompt Miss Neura with that topic, and it will provide you with a detailed article to add to your vault.

```markdown
# The Legend of Amaterasu

Content detailing the story of Amaterasu, the sun goddess in Japanese mythology, and her significance in the Shinto religion.

## Connection to Popular Culture
📍 *Okami* is a video game that features Amaterasu as the main character, depicting her quest to defeat Orochi, the eight-headed serpent.

## Tags
- #Amaterasu
- #Shinto
- #JapaneseDeities
```

By following these steps, you can start building a rich and interconnected knowledge graph within Obsidian. Miss Neura serves as your curiosity agent, helping you fill knowledge gaps based on your intrinsic interests. The more content you add to your vault, the more valuable it becomes as a resource for learning and research.
