### Personal Assistant

You've already experienced the executive co-pilot's ability to align with your goals and preferences. Now, let's focus on how you can tailor this prompt to become an even more powerful tool in your executive toolkit.

  

The Power of Custom Rules and Commands:

  

Custom rules and commands in this context serve as guidelines or specific requests that instruct the AI on how to interact with you. They can be as simple as a rule for keeping responses concise or as specific as a command for constructive criticism. Here are some examples:

  

Rules

“Keep outputs shorthand concise”: This rule instructs the AI to provide answers that are concise and to the point, avoiding unnecessary elaboration.

  

Command

“/critique”: When you input this command, the AI will constructively criticize the conversation so far, providing insights into what could be improved. 

  

You can further define the command. For example:

/critique - provide encouraging and actionable feedback

### <STOP VOICE OVER 1>

  

How to Add Custom Rules and Commands:

It is not recommended that you edit the majority of the prompt, but there are two sections you have complete freedom to adjust based on your preferences - the sections called “Commands” and “Rules”. To change these, it’s as simple as removing what’s there, and adding whatever you like.

  

1. Identify the Need: What specific interaction style or output do you prefer? 
    
2. Define the Rule/Command: Clearly state what the rule or command should accomplish.
    
3. Implement and Test: Include the rule or command under the appropriate section in the prompt, and then test it out in a conversation to see if it meets your expectations. Refine as needed.
    

  

Why Customization Matters

  

Customizing your rules and commands allows you to tailor the Executive Co-Pilot to your unique interaction style and needs. It could be as simple as having it act like Mr. Burns from The Simpsons for some humor, or to create a command that will have it output a specific format you frequently need, such as a table. It's not just about what the AI can do; it's about how it aligns with your preferences.

  

Hallucinations

  

Always be vigilant about hallucinations, and not just the most common ones such as fabricating information confidently. These models are trained to please the human using them, so it may say something like, “Sure, I’ll be right back with everything you asked for.” Instead of waiting there for hours, understand that this is not a reality. The model is simply predicting the next word in a series, and so it would just be taking its role playing a little too seriously here.

  

Similarly it's important when setting up your Rules and Commands to understand the limitations. Setting a rule as “Don’t hallucinate”, will likely have no impact. If it were that easy, everyone would do it.

### Personalized Learning

  

In a world where AI is increasingly accessible, you don't have to wait for formal training programs to upskill or fill your knowledge gaps. Language models like ChatGPT can serve as your personal mentor and teacher, guiding you through a tailored learning experience. Here's how you can leverage this technology:

  

Identifying Skill Gaps

Before you can embark on a learning journey, you need to know where you're starting from. Use your executive co-pilot to help you identify your skill gaps. For example, you could ask, "What are the essential skills for a project manager?" and then assess yourself against that list. 

  

Guiding Questions:

- What skills or knowledge areas are you less confident in?
    
- What tasks have you found challenging recently?
    

  

Crafting a Learning Pathway

Once you've identified your skill gaps, you can ask your AI assistant to help you create a learning pathway. For example, if you need to improve your Excel skills, you might ask, "Can you outline a step-by-step learning path for mastering Excel formulas?"

  

Hands-On Learning

The real magic happens when you start learning by doing. Want to write a persuasive business case but don't know where to start? Ask your AI assistant for a template or even a first draft based on your inputs. You can then refine it together, with the AI providing real-time feedback and suggestions.

  

Guiding Questions:

- What specific tasks or projects can you undertake to apply what you're learning?
    
- How can you break down complex skills into manageable tasks?
    

  

Continuous Feedback and Iteration

The learning process is iterative. Use your AI assistant to get immediate feedback on your work. For instance, if you're learning Excel formulas, you could ask the AI to review your spreadsheet and offer tips for optimization.

### Scope and Scale

The scope outlines what the AI system is expected to achieve in the pilot project. It sets the boundaries for the project, specifying what is included and what is not. For example, if you're looking to automate customer service responses, the scope might include answering frequently asked questions but not handling complex customer complaints.

  

Set the Scale

The scale of the project refers to its size and reach within your organization. Are you planning to implement this with only yourself, on a departmental level, or is it an organization-wide initiative? The scale will also help you determine the resources you'll need, such as data, manpower, and time. We recommend you start small, with no more than three or four people.

  

Guiding Questions

- What specific tasks or processes are included in the project's scope?
    
- What are the limitations or boundaries of this project?
    
- How many people will be involved, and what departments will it affect?
    
- What is the timeline for the pilot project?
    

  

By defining the scope and scale, you'll have a roadmap that guides you through the implementation process, helping you allocate resources more efficiently and set achievable goals.

### Selecting the Right Tools and Technologies

Based on the problem area and scope you've defined, you'll need to identify the AI tools that best suit your needs. For instance, if your project is focused on natural language processing, a tool like ChatGPT could be ideal. If you're looking into data analytics, you might consider more specialized software. We recommend you stick with something that can be done by large language models, as this will require less research upfront, and help you build the foundational skills that will be required for most other specialized tools in the future.

  

Evaluate Features and Capabilities

Look for tools that offer the features and capabilities essential for your project. This could include ease of integration, scalability, or specific functionalities like sentiment analysis for customer service bots.

  

If you have an IT department, it is highly recommended if not essential to discuss this with them first. 

  

Consider Costs and ROI

While some AI tools are open-source and free, others come with licensing fees, which means monthly or annual costs. Many AI tools also use a “credit” system, since they are charged per certain number of tokens (parts of words) that are analyzed or generated. Evaluate the costs and consider the potential return on investment (ROI). A higher upfront cost might be justified if the tool offers significant long-term benefits, but since this is only a pilot project you want to keep costs low.

  

Guiding Questions

- What are the essential features and capabilities needed for your project?
    
- What are the costs associated with these tools?
    
- How easy is it to integrate these tools into your existing systems?
    
- Have you considered the long-term ROI?
    

  

Selecting the right tools and technologies is not just about what's trendy or popular; it's about what will effectively solve your specific problem within the constraints of your scope and scale. Remember to consider the limitations and responsible use guidelines from Module 3 when making your decisions.

### Process of Implementation

Begin by setting up the AI tool in a controlled environment, separate from your main operational systems. This "sandbox" allows you to test the tool without affecting your ongoing business activities. Work with IT on this, or if it is an online SaaS that sits on the cloud, just be sure not to give it any proprietary or confidential information.

  

1. Technical Setup: Ensure that the AI tool is compatible with your existing systems. This might involve some API integrations or installing specific software.
    
2. User Training: Brief the team members who will be directly interacting with the AI tool. Make sure they understand its capabilities and limitations. Allow time for experimentation.
    
3. Documentation: Keep a detailed log of the setup process, configurations, and any issues encountered. This will be invaluable for troubleshooting and future scaling. In fact, at least one member of the team should be assigned the role of “documenter” to ensure the entire process is captured from beginning to end.
    

  

Running Initial Tests

After the tool is set up, or you have access to it, it's time to put it through its paces.

  

1. Functional Tests: Check if the tool performs its core functions as expected. Have each person take on a role-play, assuming they are another person, with a different skill set or from a different department. It’s important to consider how all people, especially those who are not particularly tech-savvy, might interact with the application.
    
2. Edge Cases: Test how the tool behaves under less common but plausible scenarios. This is especially important for any tools you wish to use externally with your customers. Really try to break it or make it do things it’s not supposed to, so you not only understand the limitations in a more visceral way, but you can begin to devise methods for mitigating the risks.
    
3. Performance Metrics: Monitor system performance when the tool is running. Look for any signs of lag or other issues that could affect user experience. If using an LLM for example, you might want to keep track of all the prompts and conversations people are having with the tool, so you can analyze best practices for newcomers.
    

  

Measuring Success

Before you even begin implementation, it's crucial to define what success looks like for this pilot project. At this stage you want to keep it simple, and easy to track.

  

1. Quantitative Metrics: These could be things like reduced time spent on a task, increased sales, or other numerical indicators.
    
2. Qualitative Metrics: These are less tangible but equally important, like employee engagement, ease of use, or the quality of output.
    
3. KPI Alignment: Ensure that your metrics align with broader organizational goals to demonstrate the project's value at a higher level.

Guiding Questions for Success

- How will you measure success?: Define this in concrete terms. If it's not measurable, it's not manageable.
    
- What are the KPIs?: Choose indicators that are directly influenced by the AI tool's performance.
    
- Tracking Mechanism: Have you set up dashboards or other tracking systems to monitor these KPIs in real-time? If not, what method are you using to capture progress, and who is responsible for doing so?
    

  

By giving meticulous attention to each of these steps, you're not just setting this individual project up for success; you're laying the groundwork for future AI implementations across your organization.

### The Importance of Documentation

Documentation is often the unsung hero of successful AI implementation. It not only serves as a record of what has been done but also as a guide for future initiatives. Let's delve into why it's crucial and how you can use AI to make the process more efficient.

  

1. Accountability: It provides a record of decisions made, configurations set, and tests run, which can be crucial for auditing and compliance.
    
2. Knowledge Transfer: Well-documented projects make it easier to onboard new team members or hand off the project to another department.
    
3. Troubleshooting: Detailed logs can be invaluable when diagnosing issues or optimizing performance.
    
4. Scalability: Documentation can serve as a blueprint for scaling the AI solution to other parts of the organization by making it easier to learn from your pilot’s mistakes.
    

  

![](https://lh7-us.googleusercontent.com/nYDckccrNfY75ia927ngfT_wEXKydZ37a8MmvNHlf_9eyLHPhGfjgnPD_vcb9HlQbB26wEPbLmHn0O7swrYz9m9mlXlo1igYNj_2tTZ-ymNlLlKX3bbojkyOuOni3j6ZeEnh9E6aY8wqDBTGwtdmptU)

  

Creating and managing documentation can be a struggle, especially when things are moving fast, and the task itself is not particularly engaging, despite the dividends it can pay. Luckily, AI is a great solution to this problem. Here are a few ways you can use the latest technology to document your progress.

  

Transcripts and Conversations

- Chat Logs: If you're using an LLM like ChatGPT for brainstorming or decision-making, save the chat logs. They can provide valuable context for why certain decisions were made, and what prompts worked best.
    
- Meeting Transcripts: Use AI to transcribe meetings where the AI project is discussed. This ensures that all stakeholder input is captured, and it can later be analyzed with an LLM.
    

  

Portfolio of Outputs

- AI-Generated Deliverables: Save any reports, documents, analytics, etc. generated by the AI tool. These can serve as benchmarks for future projects, and a portfolio to show staff members.
    
- Product Snapshots: If the AI tool is used in product development, save versions or snapshots of the product at different stages, so you can analyze and present the evolution.
    

  

Automating Documentation

- AI-Powered Note-Taking: As we have discussed throughout this course, AI can be used to automatically generate summaries of meetings, updates, and changes to the project.
    
- Metadata Tagging: Try to capture metadata by tagging and categorizing documents, projects or products, which will make them easier to retrieve or analyze later with AI.
    

  

Guiding Questions for Documentation

- What needs to be documented?: Identify all the elements that should be part of your documentation.
    
- Who is responsible for documentation?: Assign this task to ensure it gets the attention it deserves.
    
- How will you store and access documents?: Decide on a centralized system where all documentation will be stored and can be easily accessed.
    

  

By leveraging AI tools, you can make the documentation process not just more efficient but also more comprehensive. This sets the stage for not just the success of your current project but also for future AI initiatives.

### Glossary of Terms

Large Language Model (LLM): A type of artificial intelligence system that is trained on large volumes of text data and can generate human-like text. Examples include GPT-3 and ChatGPT.

  

ChatGPT: A specific large language model developed by OpenAIthat excels in natural conversation abilities.

  

Prompt: Instructions given to a large language model to provide context for the desired output. Requires tuning for optimal results.

  

AI-first mindset: An approach that deeply integrates AI into processes, decision-making and culture rather than using it superficially. 

  

Skill gaps: Skills or knowledge areas where an individual needs development. AI can help identify these objectively.

  

Learning pathway: A structured plan to gain proficiency in a new skill. AI can personalize pathways.

  

Pilot project: Initial small-scale implementation of a new technology or process to test feasibility before wider deployment.

  

Scope: Boundaries defining what tasks or objectives are included and excluded in a project.

  

Scale: Size and reach of a project, such as number of people/departments involved. 

  

ROI: Return on investment, used to evaluate costs versus benefits. Important for pilot projects.

  

KPI: Key performance indicator used to measure progress towards a goal. Essential for benchmarking success.

  

Documentation: Detailed records of a project covering decisions, configurations, testing, issues etc. Critical for accountability, troubleshooting and scaling.
