## Map of Contents in a Digital Garden Using Obsidian

### Introduction
A Map of Contents in [[Synthbrain/02 -🧙🏿‍♂️ Learning Lab/🚶🏽‍♀️ Walkthroughs/Second Brain Tutorials/Concepts/Obsidian]] for a Digital Garden acts as a dynamic, organized index, guiding through the evolving landscape of ideas and notes.

### Creating a Map of Contents
- **Identify Key Themes**: Determine the main categories or themes that encapsulate your garden's content.
- **Central Hub Note**: Create a central note in Obsidian that serves as the Map of Contents, linking to various themes and key ideas.
- **Linking for Navigation**: Utilize Obsidian's linking capabilities to connect individual notes to their respective categories in the Map of Contents.
- **Evolving Structure**: Regularly update the Map of Contents to reflect the growth and changes within your Digital Garden.

### Benefits
- **Enhanced Organization**: Provides a clear structure and path through your content.
- **Easier Navigation**: Facilitates quick access to different areas of your garden.
- **Reflects Growth**: Adapts to the evolving nature of your garden, capturing new insights and connections.

### Critique
While beneficial for organization, creating a Map of Contents requires ongoing maintenance. It might also constrain the free-form nature of a Digital Garden, where serendipitous discovery is a key element.
