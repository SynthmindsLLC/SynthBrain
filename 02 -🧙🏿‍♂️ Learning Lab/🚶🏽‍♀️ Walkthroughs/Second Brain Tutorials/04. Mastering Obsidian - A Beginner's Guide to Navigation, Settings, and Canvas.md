## Navigating Obsidian's Interface

Obsidian's interface is split into two main sidebars. The left sidebar, which we've previously discussed, and the right sidebar, which houses several useful features:

- **Switcher**: Quickly switch between notes.
- **Graph View**: Visualize connections between your notes.
- **Canvas**: Create visual mind maps and workflows (more on this soon).
- **Daily Note**: Access today's note for daily logging (we'll cover this in our next session).
- **Templates**: Set up templates for repetitive structures in your notes.
- **Command Palette**: Access all Obsidian commands in one place.

### Command Palette and Hotkeys

The command palette is a powerhouse tool in Obsidian. You can access it by clicking the icon on the right sidebar or using the hotkey `Ctrl + P`. It's a quick way to perform actions without navigating through menus. For example, you can export to PDF, toggle light/dark mode, or open another vault.

Hotkeys are another way to speed up your workflow. For instance, `Ctrl + ,` opens the settings menu. As you become more familiar with Obsidian, you'll find hotkeys invaluable for efficiency.

## Delving into Settings

Obsidian's settings can be overwhelming at first, but they're crucial for tailoring the app to your needs. Here's a brief overview of what you can find in the settings:

- **Editor**: Customize your writing experience (most users stick with the defaults).
- **Files and Links**: Set the default location for new notes and ensure Wikilinks are enabled.
- **Appearance**: Choose between dark and light mode, select themes, and adjust fonts.
- **Hotkeys**: Assign custom hotkeys to actions you frequently use.

Take your time to explore these settings and adjust them as you become more comfortable with Obsidian.

## Introducing the Canvas Feature

Canvas in Obsidian is like a simplified Miro board, perfect for creating workflows or mind maps. Here's how to get started:

1. Access the canvas by clicking the icon on the right sidebar or using the command palette (`Ctrl + P` and type "canvas").
2. Double-click anywhere on the canvas to create a new box.
3. Customize the box with colors, labels, and connect them with arrows to indicate flow or relationships.
4. Drag and drop notes from your vault onto the canvas to integrate them into your visual map.
5. Utilize backlinks and tags within the canvas to maintain connections between your ideas.

Here's a simple markdown example to illustrate how you might describe creating a workflow on the canvas:

```markdown
# Creating a Workflow on Obsidian Canvas

1. **Open the Canvas**: Access it from the right sidebar or with `Ctrl + P`.
2. **Create a Box**: Double-click on the canvas and enter "Workflow 1".
3. **Customize**: Change the color or edit the box as needed.
4. **Add Another Box**: Double-click elsewhere and label it "Workflow 2".
5. **Connect Boxes**: Click and drag from the dot on one box to another to create an arrow.
6. **Label Arrows**: Double-click on an arrow to add a label like "Process".
7. **Integrate Notes**: Drag a note from your vault onto the canvas to link it to your workflow.
8. **Use Backlinks and Tags**: Rename a box to a note title to create a backlink.
```
