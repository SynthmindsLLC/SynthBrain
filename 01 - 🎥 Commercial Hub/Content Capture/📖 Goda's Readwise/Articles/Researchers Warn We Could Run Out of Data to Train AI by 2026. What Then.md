- Why high-quality data are important for AI ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hnd0x07y5jr49d0ggm70d97h))
- research shows online data stocks are growing much slower than datasets used to train AI. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hnd0xt4mrz0tbwaq72hjfh2t))
- [a group of researchers](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.04325.pdf) predicted we will run out of high-quality text data before 2026 if the current AI training trends continue. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hnd0y5ay28nb6b77w5nt2mye))
- They also estimated low-quality language data will be exhausted sometime between 2030 and 2050, and low-quality image data between 2030 and 2060. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hnd0ydd20n5c3bpwbbr00s0t))
- It’s likely in the coming years they will be able to train high-performing AI systems using less data, and possibly less computational power. This would also help reduce AI’s [carbon footprint](https://earth.org/the-green-dilemma-can-ai-fulfil-its-potential-without-harming-the-environment/). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hnd0zqqp8a4b697379g2kx8t))
- Another option is to use AI to create [synthetic data](https://www.forbes.com/sites/robtoews/2022/06/12/synthetic-data-is-about-to-transform-artificial-intelligence/?sh=1ac7daa37523) to train systems. In other words, developers can simply generate the data they need, curated to suit their particular AI model. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hnd104y8jtmh8symhrnf5efp))
- Several projects are already using synthetic content, often sourced from data-generating services such as [Mostly AI](https://mostly.ai/synthetic-data/why-mostly-ais-synthetic-data-platform). This will [become more common](https://www.wsj.com/articles/fake-it-to-make-it-companies-beef-up-ai-models-with-synthetic-data-11627032601) in the future. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hnd10fats9q7xh4kenrg8qjv))
- Developers are also searching for content outside the free online space, such as that held by large publishers and offline repositories. Think about the millions of texts published before the internet. Made available digitally, they could provide a new source of data for AI projects. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hnd111z3bqpzbq0z2na11bvg))
- News Corp, one of the world’s largest news content owners (which has much of its content behind a paywall) recently said it was [negotiating](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/news-corp-negotiations-with-ai-companies-over-content-usage-ceo-2023-09-07/) content deals with AI developers. Such deals would force AI companies to pay for training data – whereas they have mostly scraped it off the internet for free so far. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hnd11j8hvs1x7d47hwfeqb7a))
