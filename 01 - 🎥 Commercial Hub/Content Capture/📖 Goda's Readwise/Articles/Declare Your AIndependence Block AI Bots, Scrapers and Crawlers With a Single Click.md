## Highlights
- To help preserve a safe Internet for content creators, we’ve just launched a brand new “easy button” to block all AI bots. It’s available for all customers, including those on our free tier. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nr5a2502e1vrf8rks8k8sb))
- although some AI companies clearly identify their web scraping bots, not all AI companies are being transparent. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nr607a39zr5qcnc024cg7n))
- Google reportedly [paid $60 million a year](https://www.reuters.com/technology/reddit-ai-content-licensing-deal-with-google-sources-say-2024-02-22/) to license Reddit’s user generated content, and most recently, Perplexity has been [accused of impersonating legitimate visitors](https://rknight.me/blog/perplexity-ai-is-lying-about-its-user-agent/) in order to scrape content from websites. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nr6k4e7jenyf8yb88y4wnd))
- Last year, [Cloudflare announced the ability for customers to easily block AI bots](https://blog.cloudflare.com/ai-bots) that behave well. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nr726ga8yawwatae55z4ra))
- These bots follow [robots.txt](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/bots/what-is-robots-txt/), and don’t use unlicensed content to train their models or run inference for [RAG](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/what-is-retrieval-augmented-generation/) applications using website data. Even though these AI bots follow the rules, Cloudflare customers overwhelmingly opt to block them. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nr7nx0qf9wgkgnbzrpa278))
- we’ve added a brand new one-click to block all AI bots. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nr8bbxzx4vbb203d4sf106))
- It’s available for all customers, including those on the free tier. To enable it, simply navigate to the [Security > Bots](https://dash.cloudflare.com/?to=/:account/:zone/security/bots/configure) section of the Cloudflare dashboard, and click the toggle labeled AI Scrapers and Crawlers. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nr8pq9aq5sva7mnth4ckzd))
- This feature will automatically be updated over time as we see new fingerprints of offending bots we identify as widely scraping the web for model training. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nr9574dgr024b26pxszdx5))
- The graph below illustrates the most popular AI bots seen on Cloudflare’s network in terms of their request volume. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nr9nq9cqkfnn4p2r9zakxe))
- we see that *Bytespider*, *Amazonbot*, *ClaudeBot*, and *GPTBot* are the top four AI crawlers. Operated by ByteDance, the Chinese company that owns TikTok, *Bytespider* is reportedly used to gather training data for its large language models (LLMs), including those that support its ChatGPT rival, Doubao. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nranr4j4mp29fq2fqwxzk3))
- *Amazonbot* and *ClaudeBot* follow *Bytespider* in request volume. *Amazonbot*, reportedly used to index content for Alexa’s question-answering, sent the second-most number of requests and *ClaudeBot*, used to train the Claude chat bot, has recently increased in request volume. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nrb7gz7sc9vdxtq5ye9c3n))
- *Bytespider* not only leads in terms of number of requests but also in both the extent of its Internet property crawling and the frequency with which it is blocked. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nrbtnsb42vry026jdsxg62))
- Following closely is *GPTBot*, which ranks second in both crawling and being blocked. *GPTBot*, managed by OpenAI, collects training data for its LLMs, which underpin AI-driven products such as ChatGPT. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nrcb0grtd612xjjegbf5am))
- While our analysis identified the most popular crawlers in terms of request volume and number of Internet properties accessed, many customers are likely not aware of the more popular AI crawlers actively crawling their sites. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nrd85p5stdeh2ez8dp08tk))
- robots.txt entries across the [top 10,000 Internet domains](https://radar.cloudflare.com/domains) to identify the most commonly actioned AI bots, then looked at how frequently we saw these bots on sites protected by Cloudflare. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nrdrtt6tb4m4rdgch8xhwq))
- In the graph below, which looks at disallowed crawlers for these sites, we see that customers most often reference *GPTBot, CCBot*, and *Google* in robots.txt, but do not specifically disallow popular AI crawlers like *Bytespider* and *ClaudeBot*. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nre9q2a0p8gzhtj9rpa78t))
- In June, AI bots accessed around 39% of the top one million Internet properties using Cloudflare, but only 2.98% of these properties took measures to block or challenge those requests. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nrfsqgaxfm2vjfa7sbkjbw))
- Moreover, the higher-ranked (more popular) an Internet property is, the more likely it is to be targeted by AI bots, and correspondingly, the more likely it is to block such requests. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nrg44qpgra62b2hsytg2q5))
- However, these blocks are reliant on the bot operator respecting robots.txt and adhering to [RFC9309](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309.html#name-the-user-agent-line) (ensuring variations on user against all match the product token) to honestly identify who they are when they visit an Internet property, but user agents are trivial for bot operators to change. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nrhbwsfc1taq6fq3evsyqm))
- Sadly, we’ve observed bot operators attempt to appear as though they are a real browser by using a spoofed user agent. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nrhxk444rv7cy09tdvx2v2))
- Take one example of a specific bot that [others](https://rknight.me/blog/perplexity-ai-is-lying-about-its-user-agent/) observed to be [hiding their activity](https://www.wired.com/story/perplexity-is-a-bullshit-machine/). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nrjp7symk69ywk9yxsnah0))
- In the diagram below, you can see that all [bot scores](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/concepts/bot-score/) are firmly below 30, indicating that our scoring thinks this activity is likely to be coming from a bot. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nrptfw9k0hfhmb0qzkw45e))
- We can see the vast majority of requests fell into the bottom two bands, showing that Cloudflare’s model gave the offending bot a score of 9 or less. The user agent changes have no effect on the score, because this is the very first thing we expect bot operators to do. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nrqqzb1p306p9d385psmf8))
- Any customer with an existing WAF rule set to challenge visitors with a bot score below 30 (our recommendation) automatically blocked all of this AI bot traffic with no new action on their part. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nrra80n02kwjahh47sm720))
- The same will be true for future AI bots that use similar techniques to hide their activity. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nrrg5ewnd6h36hsjfdbbcp))
- When bad actors attempt to crawl websites at scale, they generally use tools and frameworks that we are able to fingerprint. For every fingerprint we see, we use Cloudflare’s network, which sees over 57 million requests per second on average, to understand how much we should trust this fingerprint. To power our models, we compute global aggregates across many signals. Based on these signals, our models were able to appropriately flag traffic from evasive AI bots, like the example mentioned above, as bots. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nrsqbmppj36y2v82pj079z))
- We fear that some AI companies intent on circumventing rules to access content will persistently adapt to evade bot detection. We will continue to keep watch and add more bot blocks to our AI Scrapers and Crawlers rule and evolve our machine learning models to help keep the Internet a place where content creators can thrive and keep full control over which models their content is used to train or run inference on. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j9nrvfm3dw9nwjezsevmcv13))
