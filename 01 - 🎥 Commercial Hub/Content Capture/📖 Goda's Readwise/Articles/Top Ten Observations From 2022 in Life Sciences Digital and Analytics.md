## Highlights
- Life sciences companies have historically lagged behind banking, telecom, retail, and other industries in digital maturity. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hret6cpsk2138v5w4vhd05ch))
- The results are sobering. The gaps with other industries are not closing, and the reported benefit to businesses is moderate. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hret87wve0e2t2m2j4q2nhvz))
- Pharmaceutical companies (pharmacos) surveyed are slightly more mature than medical technology (medtech) companies: 55 percent of pharma DnA leaders say they have deployed some applications at scale, compared with 34 percent of medtech DnA leaders. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hret9bts8crz679drwjat34d))
- ***2. Life sciences companies are starting to capture value from digital and analytics but are still just scratching the surface.*** Life sciences DnA leaders surveyed estimate that digital and analytics drove a 5 to 15 percent bottom-line improvement in specific pockets of their functional areas over the past five years, yielding an annual global impact of $6 billion to $9 billion. This compares with an estimated $130 billion to $190 billion that the full application of digital solutions and innovation along the life sciences value chain could bring. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretbdsckq1b1cgq0f7r3x43))
- The largest opportunities DnA could help unlock are derisking drug discovery, accelerating clinical trials, and reinventing engagement with HCPs. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretbshtfbnfff29n5j9d608))
- ***3. Early innovation is clearly catching up, but it took the top 40 life sciences companies until 2021 to overtake the big three tech companies (Alphabet, Amazon, and Apple).*** The top 20 pharmacos and the top 20 medtech companies generated more than 1,700 DnA and life sciences–related patents in 2021, a growth surge of more than 70 percent over 2017 that finally allowed the industry to overtake the combined volumes of Alphabet, Amazon, and Apple. Publications also increased by 28 percent in that period, reaching 650 last year. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretdfj9pjkhk3wp5gwdwctx))
- there is not yet a clear correlation between life sciences companies’ digital maturity and their financial performance. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hrete3za8rxkby8gm14j3bga))
- because of long product development timelines and limited short-term benefits, especially in R&D, underlining the importance of defining and linking digital strategy to scientific and business value at every level of the organization. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretej65s83w2gk168mhcnwr))
- Funding along the life sciences value chain jumped from $5 billion in 2019 to more than $20 billion in 2021 ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretf7v4jsptvf29ehtc1j3a))
- 45 percent of the funding in commercial solutions. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretfgmb3d2410zd6htr6kd8))
- Funding in research and development ranked second, totaling $6.4 billion, with almost equal interest from investors in research and early development ($3 billion) and development, regulatory, and safety ($3.4 billion). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretfz91x26kgwv18je2wf54))
- ![US funding along the digital healthcare value chain peaked during the height of the COVID-19 pandemic and is stabilizing at about twice its historical base.](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/industries/life%20sciences/our%20insights/top%20ten%20observations%20from%202022%20in%20life%20sciences%20digital%20and%20analytics/svgz-digitalanalytics-ex1.svgz?cq=50&cpy=Center) ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretgg6qq77w0w64dyve1206))
- Year-to-date funding and digital-health stock indexes, such as the MSCI ACWI IMI Digital Health Index, for 2022 have almost halved compared with 2021, partially because of a general public-market correction. However, they are still twice their 2019 levels. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hreth4yztshsy5ybfmzqz6xn))
- The US life sciences sector still leads Europe and Asia in DnA investments. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hrethbrmbh2qwm8j3nw95qat))
- DnA-related major deals in the United States than in the European Union over the past five years—32 versus 26— but on average, the US deals were about five times larger, $700 million versus $150 million in the European Union. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretj1hsan1awg4q2bgx33a0))
- Big tech players can leverage their ability to generate cash flow to fuel their M&A activities, while life sciences companies tend to prioritize asset acquisitions over DnA deals. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretk6smggykpc2m43xarxrx))
- Hiring is generally more robust in R&D and commercial, and it’s expected to remain so. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretm70h4ybak35y04wdnam8))
- Similarly, leaders expect to grow their DnA spending by another 10 to 15 percent over the next three years, starting from today’s baseline of 8 to 12 percent of their function’s budgets. Only 7 percent of leaders expect to increase their investment by more than 30 percent over the next three years. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretmt00sa8hpv335w8vs4hs))
- ***6. Analytics rule the world of life sciences tech investments.*** Life sciences companies make 45 percent of their tech investments in [three analytics-related technologies](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-digital/our-insights/the-top-trends-in-tech)—applied artificial intelligence (AI), industrialized machine learning (ML), and cloud and edge computing—and expect to derive most of their short- to medium-term benefits from them. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretnbv0b7dy4d0t9459978f))
- Applied AI has permeated the life sciences value chain, allowing more business decisions to be made based on data the organization has generated or acquired. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretnnxe802vf4rb5jbpvw05))
- Cloud technologies are increasingly being embraced by life sciences to speed tech-enabled business transformations; in 2021, more than 80 percent of the top 20 global pharma and medtech companies were operating in the cloud. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretpbgprc18veg94vhnsme3))
- companies are increasingly leveraging the cloud to shift IT infrastructure management and to fill gaps in their software development and analytics capabilities, buying software as a service rather than building and operating it themselves. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretpvtpvcg0zyf9zv87bbcz))
- Despite the high media attention, longer-term bets such as quantum technologies, Web3, advanced connectivity, and immersive reality together account for only about 15 percent of life sciences tech investments. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretqa6m84qsbzzr3rrvt7w0))
- For instance, life sciences companies could accelerate drug discovery, create more personalized treatments, and optimize treatment plans for patients by leveraging a combination of Al and ML, cloud computing, quantum technologies, and bioengineering. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hretr3hc3cbayfw6ctpx63ve))
