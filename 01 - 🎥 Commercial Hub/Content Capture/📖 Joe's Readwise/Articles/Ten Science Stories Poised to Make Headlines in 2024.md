## Highlights
- El Niño may extend record heat
  An El Niño warming in the eastern Pacific Ocean is likely to grow stronger over the next few months and might help push the average global surface temperature 1.5°C above preindustrial levels for the first time. Part of a regularly oscillating climate pattern, the El Niño is expected to worsen drought in the Amazon and Australia. The shift, which began last year, is suspected of helping make 2023 the hottest year in modern history, as temperatures in the first 11 months averaged more than 1.4°C above preindustrial levels—higher than rising emissions of greenhouse gases alone can explain. El Niño has suppressed the ocean’s ability to absorb heat, and a drop in Sun-blocking pollution also played a role—effects that will continue this year.
