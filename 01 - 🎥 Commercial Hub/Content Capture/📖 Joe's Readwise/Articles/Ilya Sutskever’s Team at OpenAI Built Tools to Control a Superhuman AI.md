## Highlights
- We believe superintelligence—AI vastly smarter than humans—could be developed within the next ten years,” said OpenAI in a [blog post](https://openai.com/research/weak-to-strong-generalization). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hhs5eyrwevpe84s6jnhxmnyk))
- Weak-to-strong generalization” is the first paper from Ilya Sutskever and Jan Leike’s “Superalignment” team, [created in July](https://openai.com/blog/introducing-superalignment) to make sure AI systems much smarter than humans will still follow human rules. The proposed solution? OpenAI needs to design small AI models to teach superhuman AI models. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hhs5fmacwgfqn35z5eewhzpp))
- OpenAI acknowledges humans won’t be sufficient to train these models, so we need to train a less complicated AI to do so on our behalf. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hhs5gk4tzytp7d990s0jg76b))
- The study found that training large AI models with smaller AI models, what they call “weak-to-strong generalization” results in a higher level of accuracy in multiple circumstances, compared to human training. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hhs5htcnw689qfyccp7anhj1))
- OpenAI says it is not convinced this is a “solution” to superalignment, this is merely a promising framework for training a superhuman AI. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hhs5j3k4a293aa7k99kg4620))
