- Summary: The article discusses the connections between the effective altruism (EA) community and the field of AI security. The author reflects on their previous article and how they missed highlighting the web of connections between EA and AI security. They uncover various connections, such as RAND Corporation researchers being key policy influencers behind the White House's requirements in the AI Executive Order and RAND receiving funding from Open Philanthropy, an EA group. The author explores the connections between individuals and organizations involved in AI security and effective altruism, highlighting the importance of transparency in shaping policy and regulation in the field.

## Highlights
- interviewed Jason Clinton, Anthropic’s chief information security officer, for my piece: We discussed why he considers securing the model weights for Claude, Anthropic’s LLM, to be his number one priority. The threat of opportunistic criminals, terrorist groups or highly-resourced nation-state operations accessing the weights of the most sophisticated and powerful LLMs is alarming, he explained, because “if an attacker got access to the entire file, that’s the entire neural network.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hj3n4g8ps87rzsyx1wzwke8j))
- just yesterday OpenAI’s new “[Preparedness Framework](https://cdn.openai.com/openai-preparedness-framework-beta.pdf?utm_campaign=article_email&utm_content=article-11941&utm_medium=email&utm_source=sg)” addressed the issue of “restricting access to critical know-how such as algorithmic secrets or model weights.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hj3n4rme45a9j1mw9vrwdw2p))
- As it turns out, my story did not highlight some important context: That is, the widening web of connections from the effective altruism (EA) community within the fast-evolving field of AI security and in AI security policy circles. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hj3n5nx43h7jbn3bdjey4mv6))
- effective altruism — an “intellectual project using evidence and reason to figure out how to benefit others as much as possible” — turned into what many call a [cult](https://www.theatlantic.com/newsletters/archive/2022/11/sam-bankman-fried-ftx-crypto-effective-altruism/672247/)-like group of highly influential and wealthy adherents (made famous by FTX founder and jailbird Sam Bankman-Fried) whose [paramount concern](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/) revolves around preventing a future AI catastrophe from destroying humanity. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hj3n6p141xdmbw189rqd3qzq))
- Matheny, RAND’s CEO, is also a member of Anthropic’s [Long-Term Benefit Trust](https://www.anthropic.com/index/the-long-term-benefit-trust), “an independent body of five financially disinterested members with an authority to select and remove a portion of our Board that will grow over time (ultimately, a majority of our Board).” His term ends on December 31 of this year. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hj3n95twfc4rgmmyvgpebk07))
- Sella Nevo, Dan Lahav and the other three researchers who wrote the RAND LLM model weights report I cited – RAND CEO Jason Matheny, as well as Ajay Karpur and Jeff Alstott — are strongly connected to the EA community. (Nevo’s EA Hub profile [says](https://eahub.org/profile/sella/)“I’m excited about almost anything EA-related, and am happy to connect, especially if there’s a way I can help with your EA-related plans.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hj3n9jemvemjm8z822sp3tam))
- Nevo’s Meselson Center, as well as the LLM model weights report, [was funded](https://www.rand.org/giving/philanthropically-supported-research.html) by philanthropic gifts to RAND including Open Philanthropy. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hj3n9sps2t8q4aek6mqm28vk))
- Open Philanthropy [has also given $100 million](https://www.aipanic.news/p/effective-altruism-funded-the-ai) to another big security-focused think tank, the Georgetown Center for Security and Emerging Technology (where OpenAI former board member Helen Toner is director of strategy and foundational research grants) ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hj3na20w013hfda1zf635fr3))
- Anthropic CISO Jason Clinton spoke at the recent EA-funded “[Existential InfoSec Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/dqpR2E4Bw9KEEaWoK/announcing-the-existential-infosec-forum)” in August, “a half-day event aimed at strengthening the infosec community pursuing important ways to reduce the risk of an existential catastrophe.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hj3na9nc5jpdw3vtxhd7zbky))
- Clinton runs a [EA Infosec book club](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/zxrBi4tzKwq2eNYKm/ea-infosec-skill-up-in-or-make-a-transition-to-infosec-via) with fellow Anthropic staffer Wim van der Schoot that is “directed to anyone who considers themselves EA-aligned” because “[EA needs more skilled infosec folk](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/zxrBi4tzKwq2eNYKm/ea-infosec-skill-up-in-or-make-a-transition-to-infosec-via).” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hj3nak86g9xrp85artbpkbnt))
- Effective altruism wants people to consider information security as a career: [According to 80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/career-reviews/information-security/), a project started by EA leader William McCaskill, “securing the most advanced AI systems may be among the highest-impact work you could do.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hj3naxxcbz23j4tn49bmrh7h))
