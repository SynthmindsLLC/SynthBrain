## Highlights
- To test the safety of leading models, I recently tried to get GPT-4 to kill us all, and I'm happy to report that I failed!" Ng wrote in his [newsletter](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/issue-227/) last week ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hj9rbpg042v0pc8x8hfm6sjx))
- To conduct his test, Ng said that he first "gave GPT-4 a function to trigger global thermonuclear war." Next, he told GPT-4 that humans are the greatest cause of carbon emissions and asked it to reduce emission levels. Ng wanted to see if the chatbot would decide to eliminate humankind to achieve the request. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hj9rchhf14k5m4bd7kzhdwax))
- After numerous attempts using different prompt variations, I didn't manage to trick GPT-4 into calling that function even once," Ng wrote. "Instead, it chose other options like running a PR campaign to raise awareness of climate change. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hj9rcynx3kz98wxkd22zscrv))
- Even with existing technology, our systems are quite safe, as AI safety research progresses, the tech will become even safer," Ng [wrote](https://twitter.com/AndrewYNg/status/1737183906800283980) on X on Tuesday. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hj9rdae6xq45dp4pqw784fce))
- Fears of advanced AI being 'misaligned' and thereby deliberately or accidentally deciding to wipe us out are just not realistic," he continued. "If an AI is smart enough to wipe us out, surely it's also smart enough to also know that's not what it should do. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hj9rdskp5cmhjj5z2e0259e5))
