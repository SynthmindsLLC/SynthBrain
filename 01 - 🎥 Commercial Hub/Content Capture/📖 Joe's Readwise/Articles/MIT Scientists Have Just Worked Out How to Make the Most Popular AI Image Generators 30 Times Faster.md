- Category: #articles
- Summary: Scientists have developed a technique called "distribution matching distillation" (DMD) that enables popular AI image generators to run up to 30 times faster by condensing a 100-stage process into one step. This technique teaches new AI models to mimic established image generators like diffusion models such as DALL·E 3, Midjourney, and Stable Diffusion. The resulting AI models are smaller and more efficient, generating high-quality images quickly. This advancement was detailed in a study uploaded to the preprint server arXiv. By reducing the number of iterations required for image generation, the new framework accelerates the process significantly, offering benefits in terms of computational efficiency and faster content creation.

([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hsynswcc8xxk0vxf3wvdjr4z))
- This framework results in smaller and leaner AI models that can generate images much more quickly while retaining the same quality of the final image. The scientists detailed their findings in a study uploaded Dec. 5, 2023, to the preprint server [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.18828.pdf). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hsynt2tva8mae2q07eb2a6cv))
- In practice, these models work by taking a random image and encoding it with a field of random noise so it is destroyed, explained AI scientist J[ay Alammar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=EXmtWyoAAAAJ&hl=en) in a b[log post.](https://jalammar.github.io/illustrated-stable-diffusion/) This is called "forward diffusion," and is a key step in the training process. Next, the image undergoes up to 100 steps to clear up the noise, known as "reverse diffusion" to produce a clear image based on the text prompt.
