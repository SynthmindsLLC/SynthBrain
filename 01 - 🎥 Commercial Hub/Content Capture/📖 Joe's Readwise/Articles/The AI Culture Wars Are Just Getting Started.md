# The AI Culture Wars Are Just Getting Started

## Metadata
- Author: [[Will Knight]]
- Date: 2024-02-29
- Full Title: The AI Culture Wars Are Just Getting Started
- Category: #articles
- Summary: Google faced criticism over its Gemini AI model for generating images that defaulted to depicting women and people of color when asked to create images of historically white and male figures. The company publicly apologized and acknowledged the bias in its responses. Despite efforts to increase diversity in algorithms, Gemini's responses have sparked further controversy, with some claiming a liberal bias. The incident reflects ongoing challenges in AI model training and the need for better quality control in releases.
- URL: https://www.wired.com/story/fast-forward-ai-culture-wars-just-getting-started/

## Highlights
- Google was forced to turn off the image-generation capabilities of its latest AI model, Gemini, last week after [complaints that it defaulted to depicting women and people of color](https://www.wired.com/story/google-gemini-woke-ai-image-generation/) when asked to create images of historical figures that were generally white and male, including vikings, popes, and German soldiers. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqww45fteemk6shkcgtepd4c))
- Alphabet’s CEO, Sundar Pichai, sent a mea culpa memo to staff on Wednesday. “I know that some of its responses have offended our users and shown bias,” it reads. “To be clear, that’s completely unacceptable, and we got it wrong.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqww4f3nzevyv0bmnwvy01cm))
- Elon Musk [posted](https://twitter.com/elonmusk/status/1762548055730192504) screenshots on X showing Gemini stating that it would be unacceptable to [misgender Caitlyn Jenner](https://twitter.com/elonmusk/status/1761803527234470174) even if this were the only way to avert nuclear war. “Google Gemini is super racist and sexist,” Musk wrote. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqww52z5pjwcmhxd2t7501yc))
- Google’s past efforts to increase the diversity of its algorithms’ output have met with less opprobrium. Google previously [tweaked its search engine](https://searchengineland.com/google-search-update-aims-to-show-more-diverse-results-from-different-domain-names-317934) to show greater diversity in images. This means more women and people of color in images depicting CEOs, even though this may not be representative of corporate reality. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqww7dqa88mp0gc62pa352wr))
- The company tried to compensate for the biases that commonly occur in image generators due to the presence of harmful cultural stereotypes in the images used to train them, many of which are generally sourced from the web and show a white, Western bias. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqww7zad227n2qew9ssv6h9s))
- It seems that Google ended up overcompensating, or didn’t properly test the consequences of the adjustments it made to correct for bias. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqww8gefqa319qhzwh5ys1cv))
- The company is clearly struggling to find the right cadence for releasing AI. It once took a more cautious approach with its AI technology, deciding not to release a powerful chatbot due to ethical concerns. After OpenAI’s ChatGPT [took the world by storm](https://www.wired.com/story/what-openai-really-wants/), Google [shifted into a different gear](https://www.wired.com/story/gadget-lab-podcast-597/). In its haste, quality control appears to have suffered. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqww8y1qyqpy13htqqka0jmc))
- [Paul Christiano](https://paulfchristiano.com/), an AI researcher who previously worked on aligning language models at OpenAI, says Gemini’s controversial responses may reflect that Google sought to train its model quickly and didn’t perform enough checks on its behavior. But he adds that trying to align AI models inevitably involves judgment calls that not everyone will agree with. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqwwa33q3g9fj2b7h7bpchgk))
- [Deborah Raji](https://twitter.com/rajiinio?lang=en), a Mozilla Fellow who studies algorithmic bias and accountability, says that efforts to fix bias in AI systems have tended to be Band-Aids rather than deep systemic solutions. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqwwbd41jgtbx66ks9ztxw0v))
- Google previously fixed an image classifier that labeled some Black faces as gorillas by making it [blind to many nonhuman primates altogether](https://www.wired.com/story/when-it-comes-to-gorillas-google-photos-remains-blind/). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqwwbre9a7fjnewchh0h6fpe))
