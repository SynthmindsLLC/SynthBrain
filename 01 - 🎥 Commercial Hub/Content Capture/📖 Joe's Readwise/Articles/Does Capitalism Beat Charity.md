- Summary: The question of whether capitalism is better than charity arises when discussing philanthropy. While capitalism has led countries like America and Japan to become permanently rich, charity only temporarily alleviates poverty. Proponents argue that instead of giving to charity, good people should give to capitalism. This can be done by spending money on desired products, investing in high-return companies, or donating to charities that promote capitalism. However, when considering the marginal impact, charity often comes out on top in terms of first-order effects. Second-order effects, such as job creation and replaceability, also need to be considered. Additionally, the permanence of the benefits and the potential for overall economic growth are factors to consider. While there may be potential for capitalist charities, the challenge lies in identifying and calculating their expected value.
