- Full Title: AI Prompt Engineering Is Dead
- Category: #articles
- Summary: AI prompt engineering has been a hot trend since ChatGPT's arrival, with humans devising creative prompts to improve large-language model (LLM) performance. However, recent research indicates that having the model optimize prompts itself yields better results than human intervention. Autotuned prompts have shown success in areas like math questions and image generation, surpassing manually engineered prompts. Despite advancements, prompt engineering roles are expected to evolve and adapt as AI models progress, with the focus shifting towards a more automated and efficient approach.

## Highlights
- new research suggests that prompt engineering is best done by the model itself, and not by a human engineer. This has cast doubt on prompt engineering’s future—and increased suspicions that a fair portion of prompt engineering jobs may be a passing fad, at least as the field is currently imagined. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hrb4tzs1h6epgf5kjb45kbaa))
- people have found that asking models to explain its reasoning step by step—a technique called [chain-of-thought](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903)—improved their performance on a range of math and logic questions. Even weirder, Battle found that giving a model positive prompts, such as “this will be fun” or “you are as smart as chatGPT,” sometimes improved performance. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hrb4vsg78nrbt72k7dp40byx))
- Battle and Gollapudi decided to [systematically test](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10949.pdf) how different prompt engineering strategies impact an LLM’s ability to solve grade school math questions. They tested three different open source language models with 60 different prompt combinations each. What they found was a surprising lack of consistency. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hrb4w8dckwdvednvy9zmpdp2))
- According to one research team, no human should manually optimize prompts ever again. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hrb4wkrsfcfga02vne5mt945))
- Recently, [new tools](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03714) have been [developed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03409) to automate this process. Given a few examples and a quantitative success metric, these tools will iteratively find the optimal phrase to feed into the LLM. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hrb4x1mg1y2k150pd37fkcqf))
- The optimal prompts the algorithm spit out were so bizarre, no human is likely to have ever come up with them. “I literally could not believe some of the stuff that it generated,” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hrb4xh2wtq0kjtkr7mf4jhf2))
- In one instance, the prompt was just an extended Star Trek reference: “Command, we need you to plot a course through this turbulence and locate the source of the anomaly. Use all available data and your expertise to guide us through this challenging situation.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hrb4xy0agdbs0bb4b2111pf1))
- Recently, a team at [Intel labs](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/research/overview.html), led by [Vasudev Lal](https://www.linkedin.com/in/vasudev-lal-79bb336/), set out on a similar quest to optimize prompts for the image generation model [Stable Diffusion](https://clipdrop.co/stable-diffusion?utm_campaign=stable_diffusion_promo&utm_medium=cta_button&utm_source=stability_ai). “It seems more like a bug of LLMs and diffusion models, not a feature, that you have to do this expert prompt engineering,” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hrb4z9a647t82ptewrnvzgh3))
- Lal’s team created a tool called [NeuroPrompts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12229) that takes a simple input prompt, such as ‘boy on a horse’, and automatically enhances it to produce a better picture. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hrb4zrnr6q82gsc8am3egzkq))
- the automatically generated prompts did better than the expert-human prompts they used as a starting point, at least according to the PickScore metric. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hrb50f24ev611gevd1k8yrz5))
- Adapting generative AI for industry needs is a complicated, multi-stage endeavor that will continue requiring humans in the loop for the foreseeable future. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hrb51npm0cj86wm8wya4ef2x))
- Challenges of making a commercial product include ensuring reliability, e.g. failing gracefully when the model goes offline; adapting the model’s output to the appropriate format, since many use cases require outputs other than text; testing to make sure the AI-assistant won’t do something harmful in even a small number of cases; ensuring safety, privacy, and compliance, among other things. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hrb52smhzs52cjzjczyda7b5))
- To fulfill these myriad tasks, many [large companies](https://www.ibm.com/topics/llmops) are [heralding](https://www.redhat.com/en/topics/ai/llmops) a new job title: Large Language Model Operations, or [LLMOps,](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/mastering-llm-techniques-llmops/) which includes prompt engineering in its lifecycle but also entails all the other tasks needed to deploy the product. Henley says LLMOps’ predecessors, machine learning operations engineers (MLOps), are best positioned to take on these jobs.
