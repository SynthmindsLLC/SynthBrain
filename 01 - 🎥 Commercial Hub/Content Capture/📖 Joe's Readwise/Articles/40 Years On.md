## Highlights
- but in the end I did discover how the Apple Macintosh got its name. Pioneering Mac developer Jef Raskin out in Cupertino, California where Apple had its headquarters, named the machine after his favorite - what’s the word? – “strain”, “breed”, “variety” of *apple*. “Cultivar”, that’s probably the right term, now that I think of it. Raskin’s favorite cultivar of apple was called the McIntosh ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hn6r1g0ab1kvtctkhs98v7gw))
- But here’s a good story to finish with. In Switzerland, a young British computer scientist called Tim was working on a program, or series of programs, that would allow all the different kinds of computers housed at the underground particle accelerator at CERN to talk to each other, regardless of their make. He devised a HyperText Transfer Protocol, (HTTP), a Hypertext Transfer Markup Language (HTML), and a program that could use and display them - what we would now call a browser.
