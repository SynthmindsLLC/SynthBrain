## Highlights
- The team, known as Whale-SETI, has been conducting research on [humpback whale](https://www.earth.com/news/humpback-whale-reproduction-is-linked-to-krill-abundance/) communication systems, aiming to develop intelligence filters for the search for extraterrestrial life. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjgef3eqd4jqh78d6knxk9t6))
- Using a recorded humpback “contact” call played into the sea through an underwater speaker, the scientists were amazed as Twain approached and circled their boat, responding in a conversational manner to the whale’s “greeting signal.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjgefenn4zymtqhgswby9c31))
- Lead author Dr. Brenda McCowan of U.C. Davis explains the significance of this whale conversation, stating, “We believe this is the first such communicative exchange between humans and [humpback whales](https://www.earth.com/news/humpback-whales-sing/) in the humpback ‘language.'” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjgefzdt7yrj4133e6xjfcg4))
- The behavior observed in humpback whales supports an important assumption in the search for extraterrestrial intelligence. Dr. Laurance Doyle of the SETI Institute is another coauthor of the paper. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjgegcj3jrpbc4d3s0gk81gj))
- Because of current limitations on technology, an important assumption of the search for extraterrestrial intelligence is that extraterrestrials will be interested in making contact and so target human receivers. This important assumption is certainly supported by the behavior of [humpback whales](https://www.earth.com/news/humpback-whales-fail-to-respond-to-vessel-noise/).” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjgegggxdjdz5ks0bjzm8g5g))
- The Whale-SETI team is currently preparing a second paper on the non-audio communicative behavior of humpback whales, specifically focusing on bubble rings made in the presence of (and possibly for) humans. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjgehcdpjwktk60ftjk44hvy))
- Researchers use sophisticated hydrophones and AI algorithms to record and analyze whale sounds.
  The AI, trained on vast datasets of whale calls and human languages, seeks patterns and structures that could indicate language-like characteristics. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjgej0qgekaendwnbds9cwb8))
