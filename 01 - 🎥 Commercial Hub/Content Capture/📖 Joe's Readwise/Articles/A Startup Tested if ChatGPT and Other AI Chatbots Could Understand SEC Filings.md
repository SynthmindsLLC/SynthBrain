- Summary: AI models from prominent tech companies, including OpenAI, Meta, and Anthropic, performed poorly in analyzing SEC filings and other financial documents, according to a study by startup Patronus AI. The models were unable to respond to prompts or provided incorrect answers. Even when they did provide answers, they were often wrong. The research highlights the limitations of AI in complex financial tasks and the need for human oversight.

## Highlights
- Wall Street financial types who hoped that AI would soon take over the tedious job of sifting through page after page of earnings reports might have to wait a little longer. It turns out AI models from some of the most prominent tech companies, including OpenAI, Meta, and Anthropic, are exceptionally bad at analyzing SEC filings and other financial documents. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjx0gcthhh9x0b7x4qrrctqe))
- The poor results mean that many tasks in the financial services industry that require lots of math and research will still have to be done by humans. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjx0gpj2psqr8zqvz3thfeds))
- For its study, Patronus AI, cofounded by two ex-Meta employees, fed large language models from OpenAI, Meta, and Anthropic varying amounts of data from SEC documents and then asked the AI to respond to 150 questions. It found that the models were either unable to respond to the prompts they were given or spit out incorrect answers, or “[hallucinations](https://fortune.com/2023/04/17/google-ceo-sundar-pichai-artificial-intelligence-bard-hallucinations-unsolved/)” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjx0hnqwrpk2rdnzj88b562y))
- For financial institutions, the need to safeguard customer information is critical, even existential, but many AI models aren’t designed specifically for combing through financial documents. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjx0mcreqs959nf50cct07yq))
- OpenAI’s GPT-4-Turbo fared only slightly better. It got the same number of correct answers 29 out of 150, along with fewer incorrect answers, just 20 total equaling 13% of the time. But it failed to provide any answer to 101 of the 150 questions, or 68% of the time. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjx0p8eh7gj2a0t0dsw6122n))
- Models refusing to answer is arguably preferable to giving an incorrect answer as it creates less risk of error, and misplaced trust, by users ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjx0pkevvbsr9hqs3ce115z1))
- In another trial of the experiment Patronus set up a different configuration of the retrieval system. This time the researchers set up a separate database for each file, rather than one database with many files, as is the case in most data storage. Here the models fared significantly better, but that system wouldn’t hold up in a real world scenario because it would require companies to create many databases containing just one file. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjx0qy95qtvfmm86m8n6qjvz))
- The AI tools all performed better when they were directly fed the entire documents needed, known as “long context.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjx0rjg7d7bq611yer6wc0hk))
- In this trial, GPT-4-Turbo got 79% of the 150 questions right, for a total of 118 correct answers, and 26 wrong answers—more than it did without the “long context.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjx0sd9m1rx5wkv3f43gtzc2))
- the same test Anthropic’s Claude2 performed slightly worse, with correct answers 76% of the time and 32 wrong answers out of 150 questions. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjx0sqxzhrk12t980tpchxsm))
- GPT-4-Turbo’s results show a clear improvement in the number of right answers and fewer non-answers, but seemingly at the expense of more wrong answers. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hjx0t90txa0tx7rrt714c9q3))
