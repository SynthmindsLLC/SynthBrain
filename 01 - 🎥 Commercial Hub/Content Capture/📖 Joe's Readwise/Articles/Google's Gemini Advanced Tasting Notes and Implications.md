- Category: #articles
- Summary: Gemini Advanced, Google's powerful AI model, is comparable to OpenAI's GPT-4 in terms of its capabilities. Gemini Advanced has its own strengths and weaknesses, such as better explanations and integration of images and search, while GPT-4 is more sophisticated in using code and accomplishing certain verbal tasks. Gemini Advanced also showcases a vision of AI as a powerful integrated personal assistant, with the potential to act on behalf of users. However, it is still limited and not quite ready to fully fulfill that role. Overall, Gemini Advanced's release demonstrates that Google is a serious player in the AI race and that other companies can build GPT-4 class models.

## Highlights
- **Gemini Advanced is clearly a GPT-4 class model.** The [statistics](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/gemini/gemini_1_report.pdf) show this, but so does a month of our informal testing. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hp6rdvm9pnt3v93ajsz9pmnh))
- Prior to Gemini, we only had one advanced AI model to look at, and it is hard drawing conclusions with a dataset of one. Now there are two, and we can learn a few things. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hp6rebr2324ey6yfk3qg0yk7))
- Gemini Advanced [does not obviously blow away GPT-4](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/steering-at-the-frontier-extending-the-power-of-prompting/)[1](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/google-gemini-advanced-tasting-notes?r=2kuc99&utm_medium=ios&utm_campaign=post#footnote-1-140794140) in the benchmarks. It is really good (more rigorous testing will be needed to figure out how good), but I would concur with the tests that suggest it is roughly equivalent, though it has its own strengths and weaknesses. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hp6rf3nhcn5eqfg206dr689h))
- GPT-4 is much more sophisticated about using code and accomplishes a number of hard verbal tasks better - it writes a better sestina and passes the Apple Test. Gemini is better at explanations and does a great job integrating images and search. Both are weird and inconsistent and hallucinate more than you would like. I find myself using both Gemini Advanced and GPT-4, depending on circumstances, as we will discuss later. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hp6rfrrkgpgpp2t6xb8n69gn))
- there is a weirdness to GPT-4 that isn’t sentience, but also isn’t like talking to a program. A weirdness only comes out after you spend enough hours playing with the AI and getting unnerved, or delighted, or both, by its unexpected abilities and seeming intelligence. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hp6rgtdk5qh6t2x6nj7eqgnq))
- Gemini is also full of ghosts.
  Seriously, if you use the system for a while, I can almost guarantee at least one moment when you stand up from your desk, walk around the room, and wonder what is going on. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hp6rhjy3wempcg3wnxspxbch))
- the “sparks” of GPT-4 are not an isolated phenomenon, but rather may represent an emergent property of GPT-4 class models. When an AI model is large enough, you can get ghosts. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hp6rjfrtjvf42waf9sqag794))
- Gemini has a much slicker interface than GPT-4, and is less prone to technical errors, at least in my testing, than ChatGPT. It also has a different “personality” than GPT-4, in either its ChatGPT or Copilot incarnations ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hp6rjxnw65670v1kgn9pnhm5))
- Gemini is more apparently friendly, more agreeable, and has a tendency towards wordplay. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hp6rk7dn26a1pszhpxyb54ct))
- Complex prompts that work in GPT-4 work in Gemini, and vice-versa… ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hp6rkh4seryb01a050fyammr))
- we noticed that, compared to GPT-4, it continually tries to be helpful. In fact, it is so helpful that it can undermine the goal of our prompts by trying to help the student, rather than letting them struggle through understanding a concept on their own. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hp6rm5avk6ka2pvtp54fsa09))
- Both systems have safety guardrails, but they trigger in different ways. Gemini seems more willing to do darker writing than GPT-4, but absolutely refuses to explain how nuclear bombs work through the discography of Taylor Swift, while GPT-4 is happy to do so. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hp6rmneyh29kaaa9zbmytnb0))
- Google may have just built the infrastructure while waiting for a smarter brain to fill it. That seems to be the case. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hp6rnsvjerdnb3t5evmqd8cj))
- With a smarter brain, in the form of Gemini Advanced, you can start to do some really interesting things that, at their best, seem magical: “go through my emails, tell me which are important, and draft replies for each,” “look up my next conference and plan a trip I would like.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hp6rp8jynf6fvav63z0547kz))
- It isn’t there yet, but it is very much closer to being an actual assistant, rather than the limited Siris and Alexas we have seen in the past. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hp6rpmzgepcmq502t4hy8kkc))
- Gemini shows that Google is in the AI race for real, and that other companies besides OpenAI can build GPT-4 class models. And we now know something about AI that we didn’t before. Advanced LLMs may show some basic similarities in prompts and responses that make it easy for people to switch to the most advanced AI from an older model at any time. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hp6rrh0fqdvh6yshr1m2qceq))
