# Let’s Not Make the Same Mistakes With AI That We Made With Social Media

![rw-book-cover](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/uploaded_book_covers/profile_1162316/gold-rush2b.jpg)

## Metadata
- Author: [[Nathan E. Sanders
Bruce Schneier]]
- Date: 2024-03-13
- Full Title: Let’s Not Make the Same Mistakes With AI That We Made With Social Media
- Category: #articles
- Summary: The risks that AI poses to society are strikingly familiar, but it's not too late to mitigate them. The biggest mistake made with social media was leaving it as an unregulated space. Even now, social media in the US remains largely an unregulated "weapon of mass destruction." We can't afford to do the same thing with AI, because the stakes are even higher. AI will affect us in the same ways that social media does, and many more besides.

## Highlights
- A decade ago, social media was [celebrated](https://www.technologyreview.com/2016/04/16/160609/remaking-social-media-for-the-next-revolution/) for sparking democratic uprisings in the Arab world and beyond. Now front pages are splashed with stories of social platforms’ role in [misinformation](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2023/05/09/trump-false-election-claims-elon-musk-twitter/), business [conspiracy](https://www.nytimes.com/2021/01/17/technology/google-facebook-ad-deal-antitrust.html), [malfeasance](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/05/ftc-proposes-blanket-prohibition-preventing-facebook-monetizing-youth-data), and risks to [mental health](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2021/11/facebooks-dangerous-experiment-teen-girls/620767/). In a 2022 [survey](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2022/12/06/social-media-seen-as-mostly-good-for-democracy-across-many-nations-but-u-s-is-a-major-outlier/), Americans blamed social media for the coarsening of our political discourse, the spread of misinformation, and the increase in partisan polarization. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry780fkvt02aygbf756hmy4))
- There is a lot we can learn about social media’s unregulated evolution over the past decade that directly applies to AI companies and technologies. These lessons can help us avoid making the same mistakes with AI that we did with social media. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry78fhk7n6068gz56ycbmg3))
- **1: Advertising**
  The role advertising plays in the internet arose more by accident than anything else. When commercialization first came to the internet, there was no easy way for users to make micropayments to do things like viewing a web page. Moreover, users were accustomed to free access and wouldn’t accept subscription models for services. Advertising was the obvious business model, if never the best one. And it’s the model that social media also relies on, which leads it to prioritize engagement over anything else. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry78pz9qd8vne3vabp0tf6h))
- Big Tech needs something to persuade advertisers to keep spending on their platforms. Despite [bombastic claims](https://medium.com/@colin.fraser/target-didnt-figure-out-a-teen-girl-was-pregnant-before-her-father-did-a6be13b973a5) about the effectiveness of targeted marketing, researchers have long [struggled](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2018.1135) to demonstrate where and when online ads really have an impact. When major brands like Uber and Procter & Gamble recently slashed their digital ad spending by the hundreds of millions, they [proclaimed](https://www.forbes.com/sites/augustinefou/2021/01/02/when-big-brands-stopped-spending-on-digital-ads-nothing-happened-why/) that it made no dent at all in their sales. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry79tmdsgxr9864wtj2bzh1))
- Google [assures](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/ai-powered-ads-google-marketing-live/) you that AI can tweak your ad copy in response to what users search for, and that its AI algorithms will configure your campaigns to maximize success. Amazon [wants](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/innovation-at-amazon/amazon-ads-ai-powered-image-generator) you to use its image generation AI to make your toaster product pages look cooler. And IBM is [confident](https://www.ibm.com/watson-advertising/thought-leadership/how-ai-is-changing-advertising) its Watson AI will make your ads better. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry7a9y557ncvxaxc1nmmkqc))
- Just as Google and Meta embed ads in your search results and feeds, AI companies will be pressured to [embed ads](https://www.adweek.com/media/why-marketers-welcome-ads-in-chatbots/) in conversations. And because those conversations will be relational and human-like, they could be more damaging. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry7arpa4fjqecjkh8036b52))
- **2: Surveillance**
  Social media’s reliance on advertising as the primary way to monetize websites led to personalization, which led to ever-increasing surveillance. To convince advertisers that social platforms can tweak ads to be maximally appealing to individual people, the platforms must demonstrate that they can collect as much information about those people as possible. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry7aykahr99mk104e2gzd80))
- A recent [analysis](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics/privacy/each-facebook-user-is-monitored-by-thousands-of-companies-a5824207467/) by *Consumer Reports* about Facebook—just Facebook—showed that every user has more than 2,200 different companies spying on their web activities on its behalf. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry7b8f6dqmyvc3bxk2rwwmy))
- A decade ago, technologists hoped this sort of virality would bring people together and guarantee access to suppressed truths. But as a structural matter, it is in a social network’s interest to show you the things you are most likely to click on and share, and the things that will keep you on the platform.
  As it happens, this often means outrageous, lurid, and triggering content. Researchers have [found](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2024292118) that content expressing maximal animosity toward political opponents gets the most engagement on Facebook and Twitter. And this incentive for outrage [drives](https://www.theverge.com/2017/10/11/16449976/facebook-political-ads-trump-russia-election-news-feed) and rewards misinformation. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry7dmy4t8tjnq8hfkk7hyxq))
- people are more likely to share false information—perhaps because it seems more novel and surprising. And unfortunately, this kind of viral misinformation has been [pervasive](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/11/23/technology/election-misinformation-facebook-twitter.html). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry7dzb89bknjc2hn6hn924a))
- AI has the potential to supercharge the problem because it makes content production and propagation easier, faster, and more automatic. Generative AI tools can fabricate unending numbers of falsehoods about any individual or theme, some of which go viral. And those lies could be propelled by social accounts controlled by AI bots, which can share and launder the original misinformation at any [scale](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/02/08/technology/ai-chatbots-disinformation.html). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry7e9znn29t3zy2y5gjg47g))
- In July, researchers at Indiana University [revealed](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.16336.pdf) a botnet of more than 1,100 Twitter accounts that appeared to be operated using ChatGPT. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry7etkg8xex80yqfgzk0w7b))
- **4: Lock-in**
  Social media companies spend a lot of effort making it hard for you to leave their platforms. It’s not just that you’ll miss out on conversations with your friends. They make it hard for you to take your saved data—connections, posts, photos—and port it to another platform. Every moment you invest in sharing a memory, reaching out to an acquaintance, or curating your follows on a social platform adds a brick to the wall you’d have to climb over to go to another platform. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry7far77a61kp39wbh4pqq7))
- Coordinating everyone to leave for a new platform is impossibly hard, so no one does. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry7g35s14acdjmkzwz98cjj))
- companies creating AI-powered personal digital assistants will make it hard for users to transfer that personalization to another AI. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry7gfs2cy9pk56wkaqwysg1))
- some people have formed close, perhaps even [familial, bonds](https://www.businessinsider.com/woman-who-married-ai-chatbot-open-to-real-world-dating-2023-6) with AI chatbots. If you think of your AI as a friend or therapist, that can be a powerful form of lock-in. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry7gwnmq9qa1vh0apx6mkm6))
- Lock-in is an important concern because it results in products and services that are less responsive to customer demand. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry7h65c96k43ywnr15y88wb))
