## Highlights
- Our world has long been filled with cameras peering out over streets, malls, and schools. Many have been recording for years. But for the most part, no one ever looks at the footage. These little devices, perched on shelves and poles, exist primarily to create a record. If something happens and someone wants to learn more, they can go back. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hhewbry2m5301rv8msd598wq))
- More significantly, many of these systems promise to instantaneously flag or even predict certain types of activity based on what a person is holding, whether a face seems to match a photo of a specific individual, and other clues flagged by A.I. tools predicting the likelihood of “suspiciousness.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hhewctn16z6c3k56zsnepgbf))
- millions of cameras in public and private spaces throughout the country are currently pivoting from documentary receptacles into digital security guards. And because most of the physical cameras were already there, this shift may pass largely unnoticed. You’ve probably missed much of what’s happened already. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hhewd96hxaj101zz53jk6ct6))
- Mike Haldas, the co-founder of [CCTV Camera Pros](http://www.cctvcamerapros.com), an online vendor of security cameras based in Florida, first rolled out the [Viewtron A.I. camera](https://www.cctvcamerapros.com/AI-security-cameras-s/1512.htm) two years ago. It is now one of his bestsellers. At first, the selling point was pretty basic: Unlike motion-detected cameras, which might be triggered by a tree blowing in the wind, these systems could identify a person. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hheweyhxksq2053fffxs7fdc))
- It’s possible “the A.I. can actually detect faces and match against a database and then take some type of alarm action,” he said. To be clear, this is not [Clearview-style facial recognition](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/01/18/technology/clearview-privacy-facial-recognition.html); that company [created a notorious](https://www.amazon.com/Your-Face-Belongs-Us-Secretive/dp/0593448561) privacy-invading tool that makes it possible to identify people by comparing their face with a database scraped from social media. Rather, customers must provide their own reference images. This might include photos of a company’s employee or a person a store believes stole from them. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hhewfnca1d6szh90emc4a84p))
- Haldas also recently rolled out loitering and crowd-density features, which have prompted similar enthusiasm. One homeowners association told him that it is using it to catch teenagers gathering in the pool house after hours. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hhewgnfz9arv8apkstb02rk5))
- Quickly, it became clear that companies were interested in doing far more than thwarting terrorist acts and verifying immigration status. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hhewhjd95ybvn3gmtpv5axz4))
