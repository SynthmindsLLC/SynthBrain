- Summary: Sam Altman's desire for $7 trillion highlights the significant costs associated with scaling AI. The cost of training AI models, such as GPT, increases exponentially with each new generation, requiring massive amounts of computing power, energy, and training data. GPT-4 already cost $100 million to train, and estimates suggest GPT-6 could cost $75 billion or more. However, the resources required to achieve these advancements are currently beyond the reach of organizations like OpenAI. The exponential nature of AI progress creates a positive feedback loop if each new generation inspires more investment and decreases the cost of the next generation. However, if the excitement and intelligence of new generations fail to meet expectations, the AI boom could fizzle out. Altman's request for $7 trillion reflects his desire for a centralized and efficient approach to AI development, but it is more likely that progress will occur gradually and decentralized over decades.

The computing capacity of the world grows quickly - [this source](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/10688/how-much-of-ai-progress-is-from-scaling-compute-and-how-far-will-it-scale/) says it doubles every 1.5 years, which means it grows by an order of magnitude every five years, which means these numbers are probably overestimates. This sort of looks like our scaling factor of 30x still kind of holds, but in theory training data is supposed to scale as the square root of compute - so you should expect a scaling factor of 5.5x. That means GPT-5 will need somewhere in the vicinity of 50 trillion tokens ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hpjmh1w41a5k8a7ptc0pnexr))

For example, you can train a chess AI on synthetic data by making it play against itself a million times. You can train a math AI by having it randomly generate steps to a proof, eventually stumbling across a correct one by chance, automatically detecting the correct proof, and then training on that one. You can train a video game playing AI by having it make random motions, then see which one gets the highest score. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hpjmjjjh6rr12099r327rwam))
