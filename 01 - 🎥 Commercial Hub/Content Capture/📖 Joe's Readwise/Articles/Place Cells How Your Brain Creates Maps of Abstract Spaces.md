- Category: #articles
- Summary: Place cells are neurons in the hippocampus that are responsible for creating cognitive maps, or internal representations, of physical space. Through experiments with rats and virtual reality, researchers have found that place cells exhibit spatial specificity, with their activity strongly correlated to the animal's position in space. Place fields, the areas where place cells are active, can be mapped in both two-dimensional and three-dimensional environments. The activity of place cells has been shown to directly influence spatial behavior, providing evidence of their causal role in constructing cognitive maps. Place cell representation of space can undergo remapping, where changes in the environment lead to changes in place fields. Additionally, place cells are not solely limited to encoding physical location, as they can also represent non-spatial information, such as sound frequencies.
