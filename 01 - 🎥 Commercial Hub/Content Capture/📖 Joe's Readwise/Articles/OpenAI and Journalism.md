## Highlights
- Training AI models using publicly available internet materials is fair use, as supported by long-standing and widely accepted precedents. We view this principle as fair to creators, necessary for innovators, and critical for US competitiveness. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hknsx1np6ctrfwxaye7fmwth))
- The principle that training AI models is permitted as a fair use is supported by a wide range of [academics](https://www.regulations.gov/comment/COLC-2023-0006-8854), [library associations](https://www.regulations.gov/comment/COLC-2023-0006-8452), [civil](https://www.regulations.gov/comment/COLC-2023-0006-8735) [society](https://www.regulations.gov/comment/COLC-2023-0006-8554) [groups](https://www.regulations.gov/comment/COLC-2023-0006-8302), [startups](https://www.regulations.gov/comment/COLC-2023-0006-7623), [leading](https://www.regulations.gov/comment/COLC-2023-0006-8719) [US](https://www.regulations.gov/comment/COLC-2023-0006-8750) [companies](https://www.regulations.gov/comment/COLC-2023-0006-8594), [creators](https://www.regulations.gov/comment/COLC-2023-0006-8426), [authors](https://www.regulations.gov/comment/COLC-2023-0006-8976), and [others](https://www.regulations.gov/comment/COLC-2023-0006-9057) that recently submitted comments to the US Copyright Office. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hknsxb7spft7mqs0kbjxchcj))
- We have led the AI industry in providing a simple opt-out [process](https://platform.openai.com/docs/gptbot) for publishers (which The New York Times adopted in August 2023) to prevent our tools from accessing their sites. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hknsxwkzhyqztwrhyjf6tj3m))
- we have measures in place to limit inadvertent memorization and prevent regurgitation in model outputs. We also expect our users to act responsibly; intentionally manipulating our models to regurgitate is not an appropriate use of our technology and is against our terms of use. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hknsynbnp9ybzjmdz6db59ew))
- Our discussions with The New York Times had appeared to be progressing constructively through our last communication on December 19. The negotiations focused on a high-value partnership around real-time display with attribution in ChatGPT, in which The New York Times would gain a new way to connect with their existing and new readers, and our users would gain access to their reporting. We had explained to The New York Times that, like any single source, their content didn't meaningfully contribute to the training of our existing models and also wouldn't be sufficiently impactful for future training. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hknszrygmb5qy8bt974adnr1))
- Their lawsuit on December 27—which we learned about by reading The New York Times ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hknt03xvqz5tt5e76mf9tfrv))
- they had mentioned seeing some regurgitation of their content but repeatedly refused to share any examples, despite our commitment to investigate and fix any issues. We’ve demonstrated how seriously we treat this as a priority, such as in July when we [took down a ChatGPT feature](https://twitter.com/OpenAI/status/1676072388436594688) immediately after we learned it could reproduce real-time content in unintended ways. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hknt0k77tsv4851bd7r2tqnm))
- the regurgitations The New York Times induced appear to be from years-old articles that have proliferated on [multiple](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/267/) [third](https://m.blog.naver.com/yhkim13/221777199209)-[party](https://www.transtutors.com/questions/i-have-attached-the-file-below-here-i-need-a-distinction-quality-assignment-since-it-5775363.htm) [websites](https://www.punkinfinland.net/forum/viewtopic.php?t=106205&start=375). It seems they intentionally manipulated prompts, often including lengthy excerpts of articles, in order to get our model to regurgitate. Even when using such prompts, our models don’t typically behave the way The New York Times insinuates, which suggests they either instructed the model to regurgitate or cherry-picked their examples from many attempts. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hknt199je9ndcbkndvp3e46b))
