# Consciousness Is a Continuum, and Scientists Are Starting to Measure It

![rw-book-cover](https://static.scientificamerican.com/sciam/assets/Image/newsletter/salogo.png)

## Metadata
- Author: [[Christian Guay]]
- Date: 2024-01-26
- Full Title: Consciousness Is a Continuum, and Scientists Are Starting to Measure It
- Category: #articles
- Summary: Consciousness is a complex and mysterious concept that has been studied for centuries, and scientists are now developing new methods to measure and understand it. Anesthesiologists have been using general anesthesia to study consciousness, but measuring the transition from one state to another has been a challenge. To solve this, a simple approach called the breathe-squeeze method has been adapted, which measures changes in conscious state without interrupting those shifts. The method involves asking participants to squeeze a ball whenever they breathe in, and tracking their squeezes using a dynamometer and an electromyography sensor. The method has proven to be more sensitive and precise in measuring the onset of sedation without disrupting the transition, allowing researchers to better understand the continuum of consciousness.

- All participants reliably synchronized their squeezes with breathing during an initial baseline period without any sedation. They then received a slow infusion of dexmedetomidine, a sedative commonly used in operating rooms and intensive care units. As brain concentrations of dexmedetomidine increased, participants occasionally missed a squeeze or mistimed it. Eventually, they stopped squeezing altogether.
