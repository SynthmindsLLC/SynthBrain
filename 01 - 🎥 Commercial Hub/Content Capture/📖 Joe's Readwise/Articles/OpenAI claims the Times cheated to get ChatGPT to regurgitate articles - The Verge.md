- Summary: OpenAI has filed a motion to dismiss some of The New York Times' copyright infringement claims against it, claiming that the newspaper used misleading prompts to get its ChatGPT language model to reproduce its content. OpenAI argues that the Times exploited a bug in its system, which it is currently working to fix. The Times filed a lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft in December, alleging that their AI models were trained on its content and that their chatbots reproduced its stories verbatim, depriving the publication of revenue and compromising its relationship with readers.

## Highlights
- OpenAI has claimed in a [motion filed Monday](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/24443836/nysd-case-612697.pdf) that *The New York Times* used “deceptive prompts” to get ChatGPT to regurgitate its content. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqpmyr4w3eme18fg01p4whc3))
- OpenAI asserts that the *Times* exploited a bug that it’s currently working to fix and that the outlet fed articles directly to the chatbot to get it to spit out verbatim passages. “Normal people do not use OpenAI’s products this way,” the company says, citing an [April 2023 *Times* article](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2023/04/14/upshot/up-ai-uses.html) titled “35 Ways Real People Are Using A.I. Right Now.” This is all very similar to the arguments OpenAI made in its [public response in January](https://www.theverge.com/2024/1/8/24030283/openai-nyt-lawsuit-fair-use-ai-copyright). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqpmzbcfme35n44qjv9gtp4g))
- OpenAI is looking to partially dismiss the *Times*’ count of direct copyright infringement “to the extent it is based on acts of reproduction that occurred more than three years before this action.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqpn0qzvgkq5j54a3cfzn964))
- OpenAI similarly [whittled down complaints](https://www.theverge.com/2024/2/13/24072131/sarah-silverman-paul-tremblay-openai-chatgpt-copyright-lawsuit) in a lawsuit from Sarah Silverman and other authors to a single direct copyright infringement claim. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqpn1fv6n3geks5e15ddjdhc))
