## Highlights
- **Francis August Schaeffer** (January 30, 1912 – May 15, 1984)[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Schaeffer#cite_note-PCAH-1) was an American [evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) [theologian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology), [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_philosophy), and [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_Presbyterian_Church) pastor. He co-founded the [L'Abri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%27Abri) community in Switzerland ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hgqs073q8n4qf9ey1nv29bcd))
- Opposed to [theological modernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernist_Christianity), Schaeffer promoted what he claimed was a more historic [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) faith and a [presuppositional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presuppositional_apologetics) approach to [Christian apologetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_apologetics), which he believed would answer the questions of the age. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hgqs0hbwxxxddwvjfvsbnct3))
- In 1948, the Schaeffer family moved to Switzerland and in 1955 established the community called *[L'Abri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%27Abri)* (French for "the shelter") ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hgr0rc3gsqd6dtkca1jq31rv))
- Serving as both a philosophy seminar and a spiritual community, L'Abri attracted thousands of young people, and was later expanded into Sweden, France, the Netherlands, Canada, the United Kingdom and the United States. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hgr0rky7vfa6weg60wrs63gc))
- Frank Schaeffer initially supported his father's ideas and political program, but has since distanced himself from many of those views, first converting to the [Eastern Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church)[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Schaeffer#cite_note-17) and later becoming a [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism_in_the_United_States) and a self described "[atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism) who believes in God." ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hgr17y834ngnvt3xtzvskkf8))
- Schaeffer explained his own apologetics and how he walked a middle path between [evidentialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidentialism) and presuppositionalism, noting that "If the unsaved man was consistent he would be an atheist in religion, an irrationalist in philosophy (including a complete uncertainty concerning 'natural laws'), and completely a-moral in the widest sense." ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hgr18zemv2bzyqjjg2naq930))
- Schaeffer explains the history of the two-story division of knowledge, often referred to as the fact/value split. He also describes his apologetics method, which combined elements of both evidentialism and presuppositionalism. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hgr1avkm1a58m08p795ajdtd))
- Schaeffer thought that Rushdoony's system would require a merger of church and state, which he opposed ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hgr1cztm9rbzr0zshbm3mngp))
- purpose is to strategize how to reach and train pastors and church leaders to focus on Christ centered principles ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hgr1wnetkd7bhaehp6v49y88))
- Its aim is to point the church back to "true-Truth" and "true spirituality" ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hgr1wx4b5pwzh2qydqm8ng2x))
- Francis Schaeffer is credited with helping spark a return to political activism among Protestant evangelicals and fundamentalists in the late 1970s and early 1980s, especially in relation to the issue of [abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hgr1xbgd0dtk2ksk2gngvrpc))
- Schaeffer called for a challenge to what he saw as the increasing influence of secular humanism. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hgr1xv27aj0e72fkgbgkd2me))
- Schaeffer's diagnosis is that the decline of Western Civilization is due to society having become increasingly [pluralistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_pluralism), resulting in a shift "away from a world view that was at least vaguely Christian in people's memory… toward something completely different." ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hgr1ymfs0jm62j5m18zsq4dx))
