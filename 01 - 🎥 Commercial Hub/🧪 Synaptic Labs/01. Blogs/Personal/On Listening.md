I've been doing a lot of listening and learning lately on multiple fronts. From AI, to ethics, to Philosophy, to Neuroscience, and I've spent many many hours with Lex Fridman and Sam Harris in particular.

For those of you who know me, Lex will come as no surprise - huge fanboy - but Sam might be less expected.

Let's start with Lex first. I personally have the mission to spread love and facilitate growth in the people around me, whomever those people may be. Lex embodies this spirit as well, and so I see him as a fundamental ally in my mission. In a time where social forces have shoved us into entrenched positions like on the scarred fields of both World Wars, we've entered a stalemate that is devolving into a lose-lose situation for all of us. A race to the bottom, and the momentum feels just outside our grasp to steer away from a cliff. 

How this has translated into most of our lives is a type of identity monolith. We have been corralled into ideological pillars, where if we believe one thing, we must believe all the things associated with that. You cannot be both a supporter of gay marriage, in addition to a stout and proud racist. For some reason these seemingly opposed ideas can coexist, but we have difficulty holding the two in both hands.

Take for example the complex relationship we have with J.K. Rowling. Who, at least from my generation, does not cite the Harry Potter series as somewhere in our top 1 of favorite books of all time. It's not really a contest.

And yet...Well you know the story, or at least the gist. The point isn't the details, but how we now have a tension between internalizing Harry Potter as part of our identity, and how that comes up against our personal values. The same can be said for the legacy of Bill Cosby, or even Michael Jackson. These are complicated emotions based on the context of these figures. If you grew up with them as shining stars, if they influenced you from the bottom up, does learning about these figures as humans with baggage change how we perceive ourselves? Are we suddenly complicit with these people if we hold their art so dear to us?

I don't know. I don't believe so...I think that's what's cool about identity. It is what you make it. Sure, we're shaped considerably by external forces, and at the very extremes of these poles of a fixed versus adaptive identities, but for those of us somewhere in the middle it may be the only thing we can control...in the end, anyway.

It was this tension that has lead me to my love for Lex. For a long time I almost refused to listen to the other side...and I mean REALLY listen. To be willing to hold onto the discomfort. Lex is one of the few who has created a space where I feel ALLOWED to listen to the other side. At times I might find myself cringing to what a guest says, and there are arguments against platforming people like RFK Jr. or Kanye West...but that is also a little of the risk of doing business in this space. In an attempt to do a lot of good, you might perform some little wrongs. The point is - Lex is trying to shape a narrative with everyone that asks "Help me understand what will bring us closer together as humans from your perspective."

It was on Lex that I re-stumbled upon Sam Harris. He's a person I've been intellectually curious about for some time, but knew he held some controversial beliefs. I guess that was part of the allure...I felt attuned to his style. 

But I didn't feel like I could engage with it because of that controversy. I was almost scared to be "infected" by potentially dangerous ideas. I am glad I waited, though...because at this point in my life I feel like I have built a strong cognitive civilization of identity, including antibodies against ideas that do meet the barrier of my personal values.

So I listened to Sam Harris on Lex Fridman's podcast, and I loved it. He had also just gotten off of Twitter, which he talks extensively on his own podcast, and the two discussed all things society and AI. 

I decided I was ready to delve deeper, and after listening to him speak with many others on the topic of AI, particularly from an ethical and epistemological lens, I ended up buying his book [[Making Sense]], which is essentially a greatest hits of his favorite podcasts. Incredible - highly recommend.

For those of you who find the word "epistemology" foreign...don't worry, I didn't either. It's the scientific and philosophical study of knowledge.
