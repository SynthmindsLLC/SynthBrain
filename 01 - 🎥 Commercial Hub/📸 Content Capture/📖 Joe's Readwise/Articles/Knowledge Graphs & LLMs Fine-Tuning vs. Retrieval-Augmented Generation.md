- Date: 2023-06-06
- Full Title: Knowledge Graphs & LLMs: Fine-Tuning vs. Retrieval-Augmented Generation
- Category: #articles
- Summary: Large Language Models (LLMs) have limitations, such as the "knowledge cutoff" and the production of false information. To address these challenges, two approaches have emerged: supervised fine-tuning and retrieval-augmented generation. Supervised fine-tuning trains LLMs by providing additional question-answer pairs to optimize their performance. It can help update knowledge but does not solve the knowledge cutoff problem. Retrieval-augmented generation uses the LLM to generate answers based on additional relevant documents from a data source, not relying on internal knowledge. Plugins and libraries, such as LangChain and LlamaIndex, can be used to facilitate these approaches.

- The same problem will occur if you ask an LLM about ***any event not present*** in its training dataset. While the knowledge cutoff date is relevant for any publicly available information, the LLM doesn’t have any knowledge about ***private or confidential information*** that might be available even before the knowledge cutoff date. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hz7708hp0008e8cgdy39c9b9))
- Another problem with LLMs is that they are trained to produce realistic-sounding text, which ***might not be accurate***. Some invalid information is more challenging to spot than others. Especially for missing data, it is very probable that the LLM will make up an answer that sounds convincing but is nonetheless wrong instead of admitting that it lacks the base facts in its training.   • Changing, updating, and maintaining the underlying information the LLM uses is easier as you transform the problem from LLM maintenance to a database maintenance, querying and context construction problem
