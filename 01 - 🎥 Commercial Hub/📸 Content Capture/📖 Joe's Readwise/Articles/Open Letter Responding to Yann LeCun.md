- Category: #articles
- Summary: Gary Marcus responds to Yann LeCun's question about his ideas on LinkedIn, highlighting similarities in their critiques of current AI approaches. Marcus points out that their views on the limitations of large language models (LLMs) and the importance of common sense and world models have converged over time. He emphasizes the need for collaboration to address the shortcomings of LLMs and advance AI research together.

- In my 2018 critique, [Deep Learning: A Critical Appraisal](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00631), I argued that deep learning as we knew it then/as it was mostly commonly used then was greedy, heavily dependent on massive data, and that it would have trouble reasoning. At the time, you argued that all this was “mostly wrong”. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hzj8qxa3g9qwgx5kjv92e1pz))
- The notion that humans are faster learners? I made that my [1999 experiments with human infants](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.284.5416.875a), which I directly contrasted with then-popular ML. The lack of world learning in machine learning ? That was THE central focus of my [2019 book with Davis](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/603982/rebooting-ai-by-gary-marcus-and-ernest-davis/). About the only thing on that slide that I *haven’t* emphasized for years is the bit about drives. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hzj8r76gve4fcc6qavc5ngeh))
- In 2019 I pointed out through [a series of examples on Twitter with GPT-2 that LLMs were subject to essentially the same critique](https://x.com/garymarcus/status/1188803198980521986?s=61): greedy with respect to data, uninterpretable, unreliable as reasoners, subject to hallucinations, etc, that they lacked world models (“they don't develop robust representations of *how events unfold over time*), and that they would be inadequate on their own. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hzj8rrm7h5894gpzjde8sf3w))
