- Category: #articles
- Summary: The Future of Humanity Institute in Oxford, founded by philosopher Nick Bostrom, has closed down after facing controversies. The institute studied existential risks and influenced ideas like effective altruism and longtermism. Critics have raised concerns about the institute's ideologies, describing them as "noxious" and akin to eugenics.

## Highlights
- Founded in 2005 and lauded by Silicon Valley, the Nick Bostrom’s centre for studying existential risk warned about AI but also gave rise to cultish ideas such as effective altruism ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwj2eq31h8yk1jkwjz0aq0h4))
- Two weeks ago it was quietly announced that the Future of Humanity Institute, the renowned multidisciplinary research centre in Oxford, no longer had a future. It [shut down without warning on 16 April](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2024/apr/19/oxford-future-of-humanity-institute-closes). Initially there was just a brief statement on its website stating it had closed and that its research may continue elsewhere within and outside the university. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwj2f3dyxdaz675yyer2e82a))
- Bostrom is perhaps best known for his bestselling 2014 book *[Superintelligence](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2014/jul/17/superintelligence-nick-brostrom-rough-ride-future-james-lovelock-review)*, which warned of the existential dangers of artificial intelligence, but he also gained widespread recognition for his 2003 academic paper “Are You Living in a Computer Simulation?” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwj2fpw53qmj82ba1mg3t0v1))
- written by FHI research fellow Anders Sandberg, which stated:
  “We did not invest enough in university politics and sociality to form a long-term stable relationship with our faculty… When epistemic and communicative practices diverge too much, misunderstandings proliferate.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwj2gxsth51q4n4rjp1yt3cc))
- In 2018 it received £13.3m from the Open Philanthropy Project, a non-profit organisation backed by Facebook co-founder Dustin Moskovitz. And Elon Musk has also been a benefactor. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwj2jc3pas6ne7hqz11w0648))
- Among the other ideas and movements that have emerged from the FHI are longtermism – the notion that humanity should prioritise the needs of the distant future because it theoretically contains hugely more lives than the present – and effective altruism (EA), a utilitarian approach to maximising global good. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwj2jxvgm848tnvzs64tma0a))
- According to the FHI itself, its closure was a result of growing administrative tensions with Oxford’s faculty of philosophy. “Starting in 2020, the Faculty imposed a freeze on fundraising and hiring. In late 2023, the Faculty of [Philosophy](https://www.theguardian.com/world/philosophy) decided that the contracts of the remaining FHI staff would not be renewed,” the final report stated. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwj2knhhz8z97qr096maxzs0))
- Fifteen months ago Bostrom was forced to [issue an apology](https://nickbostrom.com/oldemail.pdf) for comments he’d made in a group email back in 1996, when he was a 23-year-old postgraduate student at the London School of Economics. In the retrieved message Bostrom used the N-word and argued that white people were more intelligent than black people. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwj2mgr98bh8azssa07sxxd5))
- the whole episode left a stain on the institute’s reputation at a time when issues of anti-racism and decolonisation have become critically important to many university departments. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwj2n4rpsrn3z4d47b5fmbcs))
- Torres has come to believe that the work of the FHI and its offshoots amounts to what they call a [“noxious ideology” and “eugenics on steroids”](https://www.truthdig.com/articles/nick-bostrom-longtermism-and-the-eternal-return-of-eugenics-2/). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwj2nx6rrvfv6w4azm3z7dcq))
- Torres notes that six years after the email thread, Bostrom wrote a paper on existential risk that helped launch the longtermist movement, in which he discusses “dysgenic pressures” – dysgenic is the opposite of eugenic. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwj2pkke38a0c8rym17ff9dj))
- Currently it seems that there is a negative correlation in some places between intellectual achievement and fertility. If such selection were to operate over a long period of time, we might evolve into a less brainy but more fertile species, *homo philoprogenitus* (‘lover of many offspring’).” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwj2q4g5yx9c1n9qx8m4p3sa))
- Just a month before Bostrom’s incendiary comments came to light, the cryptocurrency entrepreneur Sam Bankman-Fried was extradited from the Bahamas to face charges in the US relating to a multibillion-dollar fraud. Bankman-Fried was a vocal and financial supporter of effective altruism and a close friend of [William MacAskill](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2022/aug/21/william-macaskill-what-we-owe-the-future-philosopher-interview), an academic who has strong links to the FHI and who set up the Centre for Effective Altruism, where Bankman-Fried worked briefly. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwj2r28bdr97hj0xkmd74vn4))
- It was MacAskill who was said to have persuaded Bankman-Fried a decade ago to seek to earn as much money as possible so that he could give it away. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwj2rcv3y9mddk65hjxvfdb9))
- If that wasn’t enough, the coup last November that briefly dislodged Sam Altman as the CEO of Open AI, the company behind ChatGPT, was attributed to company board members who were supporters of EA. Altman’s speedy return was seen as a defeat for the EA community, and, says Torres, “has seriously undermined the influence of EA/longtermism within Silicon Valley”.
