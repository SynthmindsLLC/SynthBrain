## Highlights
- The AI search startup Perplexity is in hot water in the wake of a *Wired* [investigation](https://www.wired.com/story/perplexity-is-a-bullshit-machine/) revealing that the startup has been crawling content from websites that don’t want to be crawled. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j1021vr17b0etkc2p5wvynsj))
- Websites can use something called a Robots Exclusion Protocol to keep their content away from web crawlers, which bots are supposed to honor, though compliance is voluntary. *Wired*, along with an independent researcher, says it has proof that Perplexity has been ignoring those codes and scraping content from off-limits sites anyway. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j102208dcwsk5j2xhbrdsjjz))
- Srinivas said. “We don’t just rely on our own web crawlers, we rely on third-party web crawlers as well.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j102270efaqqv2v87emgasnb))
- Srinivas said the mysterious web crawler that *Wired* identified was not owned by Perplexity, but by a third-party provider of web crawling and indexing services. Srinivas would not say the name of the third-party provider, citing a Nondisclosure Agreement. Asked if Perplexity immediately called the third-parter crawler to tell them to stop crawling *Wired* content, Srinivas was non-committal. “It’s complicated,” he said. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j1022cwshg4r5mqsy26xbvc2))
- Srinivas also noted that the Robot Exclusion Protocol, which was first proposed in 1994, is “not a legal framework.” He suggested that the emergence of AI requires a new kind of working relationship between content creators, or publishers, and sites like his. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j1022jcbpjpfm1w98zbq8f57))
- Earlier in June, [*Forbes* accused Perplexity of stealing](https://www.forbes.com/sites/randalllane/2024/06/11/why-perplexitys-cynical-theft-represents-everything-that-could-go-wrong-with-ai/) its content. Perplexity had released a [new product called “Pages”](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/perplexity-pages) in May that lets a user create an article or blog post based on a series of questions they’ve asked the answer engine, or based on a single prompt on a specific subject. Users can add AI-generated or uploaded images, then tweak the text or add formatting before publishing to the web. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j10230zxgd6kysdcj33hp29b))
- One of Perplexity’s own Pages used content from [a *Forbes* scoop](https://www.forbes.com/sites/sarahemerson/2024/06/06/eric-schmidt-is-secretly-testing-ai-military-drones-in-a-wealthy-silicon-valley-suburb/#:~:text=An%20unrelated%20charity%20called%20White,his%20drone%20project%20in%20stealth.) but didn’t credit the publisher. Perplexity even created an AI-voiced podcast based on the *Forbes* reporting, but again didn’t credit the site. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j1023d1hzgqdeqebsg8cfaac))
- Being fastidious about citing sources has been one of the Perplexity’s core principles since launch—which made the potential omission of citations in the Pages product even more glaring. Srinivas told *Fast Company* that after *Forbes* raised the issue, his company immediately pushed out an update to Pages that puts attributions within the text of the generated article. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j1023p92q97kndb9b4vgjepd))
- Srinivas frequently says that his product will only be good as the internet ecosystem that it draws from. “We are happy creating a less-market cap, lower-margin business, as long as we are profitable and successful—and [we] make sure that the whole internet wins,” he [told the audience](https://www.fastcompany.com/91125423/perplexity-ceo-aravind-srinivas-most-innovative-companies-gala-2024) at a *Fast Company’s* Most Innovative Companies Gala in May. “Perplexity would be useless if people were not able to create new content on the web.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j1024dfjcqb2sx18cy5bjtdv))
