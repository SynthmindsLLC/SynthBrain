## Highlights
- Social media is abuzz with examples of Google’s new AI Overview product saying weird stuff, from telling users to [put glue on their pizza](https://www.theverge.com/2024/5/23/24162896/google-ai-overview-hallucinations-glue-in-pizza) to [suggesting they eat rocks](https://www.reddit.com/r/google/comments/1cziil6/a_rock_a_day_keeps_the_doctor_away/). The messy rollout means Google is racing to manually disable AI Overviews for specific searches as various memes get posted, which is why users are seeing [so many of them disappear](https://www.threads.net/@reckless1280/post/C7VVgb9Ik--) shortly after being posted to social networks. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hys5mfp85btnt3521fyqyk77))
- It’s an odd situation, since Google has been testing AI Overviews for a year now — the feature launched in beta [in May 2023 as the Search Generative Experience](https://www.theverge.com/2023/5/10/23717120/google-search-ai-results-generated-experience-io) — and CEO Sundar Pichai has said the company served over a billion queries in that time. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hys5mw64vhh15972sye17twt))
- “A company once known for being at the cutting edge and shipping high-quality stuff is now known for low-quality output that’s getting meme’d,” one AI founder, who wished to remain anonymous, told *The Verge*. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hys5nft4x07xbbjtde3srvs3))
- Google continues to say that its AI Overview product largely outputs “high quality information” to users. “Many of the examples we’ve seen have been uncommon queries, and we’ve also seen examples that were doctored or that we couldn’t reproduce,” Google spokesperson Meghann Farnsworth said in an email to *The Verge*. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hys5nyfz5e9rfj5p5f31zrg5))
- Gary Marcus, an AI expert and an emeritus professor of neural science at New York University, told *The Verge* that a lot of AI companies are “selling dreams” that this tech will go from 80 percent correct to 100 percent. Achieving the initial 80 percent is relatively straightforward since it involves approximating a large amount of human data, Marcus said, but the final 20 percent is extremely challenging. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hys5pnkmsbbhg4pph8y4vr6z))
- Marcus and Meta’s AI chief Yann LeCun [both agree](https://www.ft.com/content/23fab126-f1d3-4add-a457-207a25730ad9) that the large language models that power current AI systems like Google’s Gemini and OpenAI’s GPT-4 will not be what creates AGI. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hys5q9r5hk7m3620seva1q4f))
