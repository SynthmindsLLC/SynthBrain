# New Transformer Architecture Could Enable Powerful LLMs Without GPUs

![rw-book-cover](https://venturebeat.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/glowing-gpu.jpg?w=1024?w=1200&strip=all)

## Metadata
- Author: [[Ben Dickson]]
- Date: 2024-06-13
- Full Title: New Transformer Architecture Could Enable Powerful LLMs Without GPUs
- Category: #articles
- Summary: A new architecture developed by researchers eliminates costly matrix multiplications in large language models, reducing memory usage and latency while maintaining strong performance. The MatMul-free models achieve comparable results to traditional Transformers by using ternary weights and simpler operations. This innovation aims to make powerful language models more efficient and less reliant on high-end GPUs.

## Highlights
- Matrix multiplications (MatMul) are the most computationally expensive operations in large language models (LLM) using the [Transformer architecture](https://venturebeat.com/ai/microsoft-trains-worlds-largest-transformer-language-model/). As LLMs scale to larger sizes, the cost of MatMul grows significantly, increasing memory usage and latency during training and inference.
  Now, researchers at the [University of California, Santa Cruz](https://www.ucsc.edu/), [Soochow University](http://eng.suda.edu.cn/) and [University of California, Davis](https://www.ucdavis.edu/') have developed a [novel architecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02528v1) that completely eliminates matrix multiplications from language models while maintaining strong performance at large scales. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j0cqypr4634v0va55rayk25g))
- MatMul-free language models that achieve performance on par with state-of-the-art Transformers while requiring far less memory during inference. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j0cqyz4tgn4xv0889wxk6gzk))
- GPUs are designed to perform many MatMul operations simultaneously, thanks to their highly parallel architecture. This parallelism allows GPUs to handle the large-scale computations required in deep learning much faster than traditional CPUs, making them essential for training and running complex neural network models efficiently. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j0cqzhmsr4gj24xqyv6k59v8))
- In the new paper, the researchers suggest replacing the traditional 16-bit floating point weights used in Transformers with 3-bit ternary weights that can take one of three states: -1, 0 and +1. They also replace MatMul with additive operations that provide equally good results at much less computational costs. The models are composed of “BitLinear layers” that use ternary weights. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j0cr0r1gzwm9ghwdy9jsateh))

- Classic self-attention (left) vs MatMul-free token mixing (right) ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j0cr167qjhv5zeb00gwazstk))
- Transformer blocks consist of two main components: a token mixer and a channel mixer. The token mixer is responsible for integrating information across different tokens in a sequence. In traditional Transformer models, this is typically achieved using [self-attention mechanisms](https://sebastianraschka.com/blog/2023/self-attention-from-scratch.html), which use MatMul operations to compute relationships between all pairs of tokens to capture dependencies and contextual information. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j0cr1n35htpzxjqnxecsadaj))
- However, in the MatMul-free architecture described in the paper, the token mixer is implemented using a MatMul-free Linear Gated Recurrent Unit (MLGRU). The [GRU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gated_recurrent_unit) is a deep learning for sequence modeling that was popular before the advent of Transformers. The MLGRU processes the sequence of tokens by updating hidden states through simple ternary operations without the need for expensive matrix multiplications. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j0cr297pmt8c2c4ef6d4kh6q))
- The researchers implemented their channel mixer using a Gated Linear Unit (GLU), which is also used in [Llama-2](https://venturebeat.com/ai/llama-2-how-to-access-and-use-metas-versatile-open-source-chatbot-right-now/) and [Mistral](https://venturebeat.com/ai/mistral-shocks-ai-community-as-latest-open-source-model-eclipses-gpt-3-5-performance/). However, they modified the GLU to also work with ternary weights instead of MatMul operations. This enabled them to reduce computational complexity and memory usage while maintaining the effectiveness of feature integration
  “By combining the MLGRU token mixer and the GLU channel mixer with ternary weights, our proposed architecture relies solely on addition and element-wise products,” the researchers write. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j0cr3em04qr05800t21k0kae))
- Ideally, this architecture will make language models much less dependent on high-end GPUs like those from [Nvidia](https://venturebeat.com/ai/nvidias-jensen-huang-says-20000-genai-startups-are-now-building-on-its-platform/), and will enable researchers to run powerful models on other, less expensive and less supply constrained types of processors. The researchers have released [the code](https://github.com/ridgerchu/matmulfreellm) for the algorithm and models for the research community to build on. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j0cr5fej02engyt67thjawh9))
