# The TESCREAL bundle: Eugenics and the promise of utopia through artificial general intelligence

## Metadata

- Date: 2024-01-31
- Full Title: The TESCREAL bundle: Eugenics and the promise of utopia through artificial general intelligence
- Category: #articles
- Summary: The text discusses the concept of artificial general intelligence (AGI) and its potential impact on humanity. It explores the historical ties between AGI development and eugenics ideologies. The paper argues that the pursuit of AGI safety is influenced by utopian ideas inherited from past eugenicists.

- In his *Republic*, Plato proposed a system of selective breeding in which members of the ruling class, or guardians, who were deemed to be superior, would be given a greater opportunity to produce offspring. The offspring of inferior individuals would “be secretly taken away by officials and almost certainly left to die, along with the visibly defective offspring of the superior guardians” [[12](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/13636/11599#12)]. Aristotle endorsed infanticide targeting “any children born with deformities” [[13](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/13636/11599#13)]. Later, during the eighteenth-century Enlightenment, some urged against “miscegenation,” or sexual reproduction between members of different ethnic groups, on the grounds that it would corrupt bloodlines and “produce disfigured children” [[14](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/13636/11599#14)]. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy4134tpc6sefynkbhz8phcb))
- The modern eugenics movement, in contrast, originated in the post-Darwinian work of Francis Galton (1869), who defended the “hereditarian” thesis that “a man’s natural abilities are derived by inheritance.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy413hcewrvsn3wzb6fr68xh))
- [](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/13636/11599#p4). First-wave eugenicists recognized two strategies for improving the “human stock,” known as “positive” and “negative” eugenics. Positive eugenics aims to increase the frequency of “desirable” traits within the human population, such as high “intelligence,” by encouraging those with such traits to reproduce more. “Better baby” and “fitter family” contests, popular in the early twentieth century, are examples of positive eugenics, as they encouraged people with “desirable” traits and “good heritage” to reproduce more. Negative eugenics strives to prevent “unfit” individuals from passing their hereditary material on to the next generation. Negative eugenics is what justified restrictive immigration and anti-miscegenation laws throughout the twentieth century, as well as the forced sterilization programs implemented in states such as California. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy415b7sydpaxs9dv65nw190))
- As Bashford and Levine (2010) observe, “the optimism of eugenics, and its aspiration to apply scientific ideas actively, was among the reasons it so frequently attracted progressives and liberals” [[17](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/13636/11599#17)]. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41613dfcjj78392rtatt2p))
- California’s sterilization program continued until 1979 [[18](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/13636/11599#18)], and the British Eugenics Society still persists today, albeit under a different name. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy416bacc6cbj1rkzzh5nrh6))
- According to Galton, poverty was largely the result of one’s inferior nature. This notion was more recently defended by Herrnstein and Murray (1994), claiming that social welfare policies were unlikely to have significant positive effects given genetically determined differences in IQ. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy4181g12f6m8gkn98egnfg1))
- Whereas first-wave eugenicists strove to improve the “human stock” by altering society-wide patterns of reproduction, a process that would require many generations to work, second-wave eugenics arose in response to new technological possibilities associated with genetic engineering and biotechnology ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy418fs3k1fm7zec908hcxsa))
- Consequently, the new eugenics claims to be “liberal,” emphasizing the freedom of parents to decide whether, and how, to produce “enhanced” offspring (Agar, 1998). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy4198faasy2209m38f8g71a))
- “liberal” eugenics — sometimes dubbed “neo-eugenics” — would have the very same liberty-undermining consequences as the eugenics programs of the twentieth century (Koch, 2020; Sparrow, 2011). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy419kfm5xc53pvwq2t05n2h))
- transhumanism, a version of second-wave eugenics that affirms the feasibility and desirability of radical “human enhancement.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41afj9w6hfk50m6dd3vjbc))
- By controlling “the mechanisms of heredity,” he wrote, “the human species can, if it wishes, transcend itself — not just sporadically ... but in its entirety, as humanity.” If enough people “can truly say ... ‘I believe in transhumanism,’” then “the human species will be on the threshold of a new kind of existence, as different from ours as ours is from that of Pekin [sic] man. It will at last be consciously fulfilling its real destiny” [[34](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/13636/11599#34)]. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41bdhwg8mpwm5kg8gwf7mp))
- the aim was not merely to create the best version of our species possible, but to “transcend” humanity altogether. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41bvc63nzctvwg8q1dzk50))
- “modern transhumanism,” as we can label it, took shape in the late 1980s and early 1990s, and combined the Huxleyan vision of transcendence with the new methodology of second-wave eugenics. Hence, advocates imagined that by enabling individuals to freely choose whether, and how, to undergo radical enhancement, a superior new “posthuman” species could be created. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41cfgmrasr9rypn7b1c9hs))
- According to Nick Bostrom (2013, 2005a), a “posthuman” is any being that possesses one or more posthuman capacities, such as an indefinitely long “healthspan,” augmented cognitive capacities, enhanced rationality, and so on. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41cthcx5gpc5cp8j2b9d1k))
- The first *organized* group of modern transhumanists was the Extropian movement. It can be traced back to the late 1980s, after Max More and T.O. Morrow founded the Extropy Institute in 1988. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41d8zrtk157yc64g67m6z7))
- “extropy” was defined by More as “the extent of a system’s intelligence, information, order, vitality, and capacity for improvement” [[35](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/13636/11599#35)], and was intended to contrast with “entropy.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41djb06c7gkbyv0hx210rz))
- five fundamental commitments of this ideology: Boundless Expansion, Self-Transformation, Dynamic Optimism, Intelligent Technology, and Spontaneous Order (Regis, 1994). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41e2f2eba18cs0enbqqamy))
- Bostrom and David Pearce founded the World Transhumanist Association (WTA), which aimed to be “a more mature and academically respectable form of transhumanism” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41e9mj89j10n8ab94np2bn))
- Around the same time that the WTA was founded, another variant of transhumanism emerged: singularitarianism, whose leading advocates included Ray Kurzweil and Eliezer Yudkowsky. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41eqwfk8qk6zwcksjmy8g8))
- emphasized the coming “technological Singularity,” which can be defined in several subtly distinct ways: first, it could refer to the point at which the rate of technological “progress” becomes so rapid that it causes a fundamental rupture in human history. On Kurzweil’s account, humans will merge with machines, inaugurating a new epoch in cosmic history. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41f6e3pxzrkk0d08zmzpht))
- Our descendants will then spread beyond Earth and flood the universe with consciousness, thus enabling the universe to “wake up.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41fexw38qts3ygwg8qg66h))
- Yudkowsky, who has described himself as a “genius,” once predicted that it will occur in 2025 — less than a year from now as we write this paper [[37](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/13636/11599#37)]. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41fxrgh1fv2cjzxdwvtqma))
- second definition of the “Singularity” concerns the idea of an “intelligence explosion,” whereby algorithms undergo “recursive self-improvement” until they become “superintelligent.” This, too, would supposedly constitute a transformative moment in human history, with the resulting superintelligence(s) enabling us to become posthuman and colonize space. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41gg9xtnyy1343pef1dcnc))
- The third techno-futuristic ideology in the TESCREAL bundle is cosmism, which has been championed most notably by Ben Goertzel, a transhumanist who participated in the Extropian movement and later founded SingularityNET.io, which aims to help create “a decentralized, democratic, inclusive and beneficial Artificial General Intelligence” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41h8pzc488wmecm7a0n8sj))

- In the late 2000s, yet another community arose: the Rationalists. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41kwthq6yx6wqgr02bd21a))
- whereas the Rationalists are primarily concerned with rationality, Effective Altruists (EAs) are primarily concerned with ethics. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41mknw16b8wcns8f75x9a8))
- EA as what happens when the principles of Rationalism are applied to the ethical domain. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41mvpndz9ftnf7wds5vqgv))
- aim of EA is to do the “most good” possible with finite resources [[45](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/13636/11599#45)], and its initial focus was on alleviating global poverty. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41n2fzrz9f2gtr7cjha89j))
- Bostrom (2003) not only imagined a utopian future enabled by radical human enhancement, but noted that if humanity colonizes the universe and creates planet-sized computers to run virtual-reality worlds populated by digital people, the future posthuman population could be enormous. In the Virgo Supercluster alone Bostrom estimates that there could be 1038 digital people, and at least 1058 such people within the accessible universe ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41nwwdhnzzbs6fbctg9h6v))
- from the ethical perspective of “totalist utilitarianism,” which has been very influential among EAs and longtermists [[48](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/13636/11599#48)], our sole moral obligation is to maximize the total quantity of “value” in the universe. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41p8jgy9vw8cp9qf1hcgzt))
- Since totalist utilitarianism bases what is morally right on what is good, this view entails that failing to bring these future digital people into existence would be profoundly wrong. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41pq9avt8yf19r5ztc4erd))
- Longtermism was born when EAs reasoned: If our aim is to do the most good possible, and if the future could contain astronomical amounts of “value,” then we should focus on the far future rather than the present (Greaves and MacAskill, 2019). Similarly, if our aim is to positively affect the greatest number of people possible, and if most people who could exist will exist in the far future, then we should focus on them instead of current people and contemporary problems, except insofar as doing the latter would influence the far future. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hy41qb363rmt7fpc715wz3na))
