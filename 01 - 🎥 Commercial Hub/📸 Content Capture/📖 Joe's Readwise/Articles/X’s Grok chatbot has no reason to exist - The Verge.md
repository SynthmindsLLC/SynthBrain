## Highlights
- Grok was pitched as fun. It was pitched as funny. And it had all of Twitter — errr, X — to tap into for relevant answers. So it was supposed to be useful and responsive, too. But after trying Grok out, I’m left with one basic question: why does it exist? ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hkrccexr7a0hksvcwvh35hn2))
- For X, it’s supposed to ease a transition away from advertising. For users, it’s supposed to offer the kind of up-to-the-minute information with which pre-Musk Twitter was synonymous. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hkrcd7pbqwa8y840vq3dx3dd))
- Grok can pull information directly from X posts (formerly tweets), linking them at the end of each answer. It’s a setup that could lead to more timely answers, particularly since it’s built straight into the X interface. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hkrcdnmj2s9by21kfxmywncc))
- My first problem with Grok was, actually, its deep interface integration with X — which ended up being more messy and distracting than useful. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hkrcex5yndx9r7kmbzhe80jj))
- Once you tap the Grok icon, you can start asking it questions, but the sidebar and the trending bar are still there. ChatGPT and Bard have sidebars that let users go back to a previous chat; Grok required me to scroll up or lose previous queries. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hkrcfb3c882fad6b86h20zcz))

- Grok is only as good as the underlying X ecosystem, and that increasingly seems like a problem. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hkrcjww8918n8bxv437y0yj7))
