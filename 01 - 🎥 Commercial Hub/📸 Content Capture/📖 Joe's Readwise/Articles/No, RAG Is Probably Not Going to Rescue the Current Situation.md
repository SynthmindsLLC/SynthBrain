- Date: 2024-02-23
- Full Title: No, RAG Is Probably Not Going to Rescue the Current Situation
- Category: #articles
- Summary: The tech industry has invested heavily in driverless cars with little return, showcasing a trend of unfulfilled promises. Silicon Valley's tendency to overpromise and underdeliver has led to skepticism about advancements in AI, such as the Retrieval Augmentation Generation (RAG) technique. RAG, which aims to enhance language models by referencing external sources, has shown mixed results, with instances of inaccuracy and hallucinated information. While RAG offers potential, experts caution that it is still in early stages and far from being a definitive solution to the challenges faced by language models.

- Investors put in over $100 billion into the driverless car industry, and so far have little to show far it. Endless promises (and empty predictions) were made at essentially no cost to those who made them. So what if Elon made bad predictions year after year? Nobody cares. - On February 14th — which is to say ten days ago, in the era before Sora, Gemini, the OpenAI meltdown, and the Gemini image scandal — Andrew Ng optimistically told the *Wall Street Journal* that hallucination was “[not as bad an issue as people fear it to be right now](https://youtu.be/-mIjwN1o7nE?si=RoKj7BTLJItCzV9q)". In September, Reid Hoffman claimed to *Time Magazine* that the problem would be largely solved with months. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqf1y5aty2rjgjjz8m4ndrcj))
- Well it hasn’t. Two massive new models came out this week, and both still make stuff up. The problem certainly hasn’t disappeared. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqf1ye3vk03kkfz8n0jqmpvj))
- The latest hope is that a technique called Retrieval Augmentation Generation [RAG], which bolts external referencing to the web on top of LLMs will solve what ails LLMs. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqf1yrbm6s5c8kcws0wvn1gs))
- As *The Information’s* Stephanie Palazollo [put it](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/databricks-ceo-on-what-it-takes-to-turn-llms-into-a-big-business?utm_source=ti_app&rc=dcf9pt) recently, paraphrasing what DataBrick’s CEO Ali Ghodsi said in an interview “By themselves, large language models that power today's chatbots leave a lot to be desired… To turn them into a truly big business, the models need ways to access real-time data in a reliable way, otherwise known as retrieval augmented generation”. Andrew Ng pointed to the same technique in his *Wall Street Journal* interview: ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqf1z8ss2cfqsw5n5zmt05j2))
- The [idea](https://www.pinecone.io/learn/retrieval-augmented-generation/) is that you tie your expensively-trained, confabulation prone LLM that you can’t afford to update frequently to other sources of information such a more regularly and cheaply updated databases or company doceuments. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqf202cmsyw2sx21pc7c3x79))
