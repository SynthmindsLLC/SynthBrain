- Wu, 27, is the brother of Neal Wu, who also works at Cognition AI. These two men are world-renowned for their coding prowess: The Wu brothers have been competing in, and often winning, international coding competitions since they were teenagers, and they have helped elevate the US national coding team to a more respectable position against its Chinese and Eastern European rivals in recent years. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry270a1mc6yjje3zhd06006))
- Sport-coding—yes, it’s [a real thing](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2015-09-25/the-jocks-of-computer-code-do-it-for-the-job-offers)—requires people to solve puzzles and program with speed and accuracy. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry27bgbczk8s6j3g6xnccfm))
- Cognition AI is full of sport-coders. Its staff has won a total of 10 gold medals at the [top international competition](https://ioinformatics.org/), and Scott Wu says this background gives his startup an edge in the AI wars. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry27ztvypg7mf0tj6gxq558))
- “Teaching AI to be a programmer is actually a very deep algorithmic problem that requires the system to make complex decisions and look a few steps into the future to decide what route it should pick,” he says. “It’s almost like this game that we’ve all been playing in our minds for years, and now there’s this chance to code it into an AI system.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hry28n1paks2wfrpjw5j1ed6))
