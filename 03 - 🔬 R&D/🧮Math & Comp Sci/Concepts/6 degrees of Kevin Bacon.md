The Six Degrees of Kevin Bacon is a trivia game that emerged from the concept of six degrees of separation, which posits that any two people on Earth are six or fewer acquaintance links apart. The game challenges players to connect any Hollywood actor to Kevin Bacon through their film roles in six steps or fewer.

## Origins of the Game
The game was conceived in 1994 by three Albright College students: Craig Fass, Mike Ginelli, and Brian Turtle. They noticed Kevin Bacon's extensive filmography and theorized that he could be connected to any actor via a chain of movie roles. The game gained popularity after the students appeared on talk shows like *The Jon Stewart Show* and *The Howard Stern Show* to discuss their creation[1][2][5].

## Kevin Bacon's Reaction
Initially, Kevin Bacon was not fond of the game, fearing it was a joke at his expense and could make him a laughingstock. However, he eventually embraced the concept and leveraged it to create SixDegrees.org, a charitable organization that connects people with causes for fundraising[1][4].

## Cultural Impact
The game has had a significant impact on popular culture, inspiring websites like The Oracle of Bacon and features like Google's "Bacon number" search. It has been referenced in various media, including TV shows, commercials, and even academic discussions on network analysis[2][6][7][11].

## Playing the Game
To play, one would start with an actor and attempt to link them to Kevin Bacon through shared film roles. For example, to connect Julia Louis-Dreyfus to Kevin Bacon: Louis-Dreyfus worked with Wayne Knight on *Seinfeld*, and Knight worked with Bacon on *JFK*, making her Bacon number 2[1].

## Educational and Investigative Applications
Beyond entertainment, the game has been used to illustrate principles of network analysis and has even been applied in fields like private investigations to demonstrate the importance of making connections efficiently[2].

## Recent Developments
Kevin Bacon has continued to engage with the game, discussing it in interviews and participating in related events. He has also used the concept to promote social distancing during the COVID-19 pandemic[4].
