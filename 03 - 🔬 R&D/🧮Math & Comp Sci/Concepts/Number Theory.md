# Number Theory

Number theory is a branch of pure mathematics devoted primarily to the study of the integers and integer-valued functions. It is often referred to as "higher arithmetic" due to its focus on the properties of numbers, especially the integers.

## Fundamental Concepts

- **Integers**: Number theory deals with the properties and relationships of numbers, particularly the positive whole numbers, or natural numbers, and integers[4][5][6].
- **Prime Numbers**: These are numbers greater than 1 that have no positive divisors other than 1 and themselves. They are the building blocks of all integers with respect to multiplication[6][7][10].
- **Divisibility and Factors**: Concepts such as divisibility, factors, multiples, and the division algorithm are central to number theory[7][18].
- **Theorems and Problems**: Number theory includes various theorems, such as the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, and famous problems like Fermat's Last Theorem and the Twin Prime Conjecture[6][7].

## Applications

- **Cryptography**: Number theory is fundamental to cryptography, which protects information in computer systems[2][5][11].
- **Computer Science**: Algorithms in computer science, such as the fast Fourier transform, rely on principles from number theory[5].
- **Physics and Engineering**: Concepts from number theory are applied in fields like physics and engineering for problem-solving and analysis[5].

## Historical and Modern Context

- **Ancient Origins**: Number theory dates back to ancient civilizations, with early contributions from cultures such as the Babylonians and Greeks[9].
- **Modern Developments**: Today, number theory encompasses a variety of subfields, including algebraic, analytic, geometric, and probabilistic number theory[5][9].

## Notable Mathematicians and Contributions

- **Carl Friedrich Gauss**: Known as the "prince of mathematics," Gauss made significant contributions to number theory and referred to mathematics as the "queen of the sciences," with number theory as its queen[5].
- **Andrew Wiles**: Proved Fermat's Last Theorem, a centuries-old problem, in 1995[6].
- **Current Research**: Number theorists continue to explore unsolved problems and develop new theories, often with implications for other areas of mathematics and science[6][16].

Number theory remains a vibrant area of mathematical research, with ongoing studies into its fundamental questions and applications in various scientific and technological fields.
