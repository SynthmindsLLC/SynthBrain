Biden releases [exec order on AI](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefing-room/statements-releases/2023/10/30/fact-sheet-president-biden-issues-executive-order-on-safe-secure-and-trustworthy-artificial-intelligence/#:~:text=The%20Executive%20Order%20establishes%20new,around%20the%20world%2C%20and%20more.) on 10-30-2023
	* [](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefing-room/presidential-actions/2023/10/30/executive-order-on-the-safe-secure-and-trustworthy-development-and-use-of-artificial-intelligence/?utm_source=link)
	* Establishes new standards for [[AI]] safety and security, including requiring developers of high-risk [[AI]] systems to share test results with the government. Directs agencies like [[NIST]] and [[DHS]] to develop tools and standards for evaluating [[AI]] system safety.
	- Calls for protecting Americans' [[privacy]] from [[AI]] risks by supporting [[privacy-preserving techniques]] and strengthening privacy protections in government [[AI]] use.
	- Seeks to prevent [[algorithmic discrimination]] and promote [[Equity]] by providing guidance to federal agencies and contractors on avoiding discriminatory uses of [[AI]].
	- Aims to protect consumers by encouraging responsible [[AI]] use in areas like healthcare and education.
	- Supports workers by developing principles to mitigate AI's negative impacts on jobs and [[labor rights]].
	- Promotes innovation and competition in [[AI]] by providing resources to researchers and small businesses.
	- Advances international collaboration on [[AI governance]] frameworks.
	- Ensures effective government use of [[AI]] by issuing guidance on procurement, hiring [[AI]] experts, and training the federal workforce.
	- Critique
		- The executive order takes meaningful steps to address key risks of [[AI]] like biases, discrimination, and security concerns. Setting safety standards and requiring testing is important for high-risk systems.
		- More details are needed on how [[privacy]] will be protected and how [[discrimination]] will be addressed. Vague calls for "guidance" may not go far enough.
		- The focus on innovation and competitiveness is positive, but support for workers affected by [[AI]] job displacement may need to be stronger.
		- Collaboration with allies on [[AI governance]] is good, but the US will need to take a leadership role in developing ethical norms and rules.
		- Overall this is a promising start that puts guardrails around [[AI]] development, but continued oversight and legislation will be needed as the technology evolves. More robust protections for rights and ethical use may be necessary.
	* [Blueprint for an AI Bill of Right](https://www.whitehouse.gov/ostp/ai-bill-of-rights/)
		* Proposed by the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy. The goal is to guide the design and use of automated systems to protect the public.
		* Key points: Sets out 5 principles for automated systems:
			* [[Safe and Effective Systems]] - Should be tested, monitored, and evaluated to ensure safety. Developers should consult experts to identify risks.
			* [[Algorithmic Discrimination Protections]] - Should not result in unjustified discrimination. Proactive assessments and testing should identify and mitigate issues.
			* [[Data Privacy]] - Should have privacy protections, limited data collection, and user consent. Enhanced restrictions for sensitive data.
			* [[Notice and Explanation]] - People should know an automated system is being used and how it impacts outcomes. Explanations should be provided.
			* [[Human Alternatives, Consideration, and Fallback]] - People should be able to opt out for a human alternative when appropriate. Humans should review errors and appeals.
			* Applies to automated systems that can impact rights, opportunities, or access to critical resources/services
			* Principles overlap to form safeguards against potential harms. Measures should be proportional to the harm
			* Aims to ensure civil rights, equal opportunity, and access are protected regarding automated systems.
		* Critique
			* The principles are broad and may be open to interpretation. More details could be provided on how they should be implemented.
			- It does not have the force of law. Adoption would rely on voluntary compliance.
			- There may be challenges balancing competing priorities like innovation, efficacy, and protection.
			- Enforcement and accountability mechanisms are not specified.
			- Rapid pace of technology development may make applying principles difficultyu
			- Overall it provides a useful ethical framework, but practical implementation will require more regulatory specifics and mechanisms for oversight and enforcement. A flexible and adaptive approach will be needed as technology evolves.
