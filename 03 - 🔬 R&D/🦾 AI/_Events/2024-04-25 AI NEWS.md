- **Microsoft Sales, Profit Beat Expectations on AI Demand**: Microsoft's quarterly sales and profit climbed more than projected, lifted by strong corporate demand for the company's cloud and artificial intelligence offerings. Revenue reached $61.9 billion, a 17% increase, while earnings per share hit $2.94, surpassing analyst estimates[1][2][13].

- **Meta's Stock Plunges 15% on 'Aggressive' AI Spending Plans**: Despite Meta reporting record Q1 profits and revenue growth, the company's plans to invest heavily in AI, potentially increasing capital expenditures by $5 billion, spooked investors. Meta's shares dropped 13% in premarket trading as concerns grew that the focus on AI could divert attention from its core advertising business[7][8].

- **AI Legislation Update - April 25, 2024**: Connecticut passed an algorithmic discrimination bill, Colorado introduced a similar bill that passed the Senate Judiciary Committee, and California also advanced an algorithmic discrimination bill. Legislation is being categorized into areas like algorithmic discrimination, automated employment decision making, AI Bill of Rights, and working group bills[3][5].

- **Avoiding AI Malpractice in Healthcare**: Researchers highlight the risks of clinicians relying on AI for medical decision-making, as it could expose them to liability in malpractice suits. Key recommendations include using AI as a confirmatory tool, improving transparency about limitations, and thoroughly testing AI devices before acquisition[4].

- **OpenAI CEO Sam Altman Talks AI Development and Society**: In a talk at Stanford, Altman addressed a crowd of over 1,000 people about his vision for the future of AI and its societal implications[6].

- **Other AI News**: Cisco signs Vatican pledge for ethical AI development[11], Georgia Tech partners with NVIDIA for an AI Makerspace Hub[12], and the automotive AI market is predicted to reach $19.09 billion by 2032[14]. An opinion piece argues for focusing on making AI useful rather than getting caught up in hype and doomsaying[9].
