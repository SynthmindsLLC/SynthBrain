## Major AI Developments

- **OpenAI's "Strawberry" Project**: OpenAI is reportedly working on a new AI technology codenamed "Strawberry", aimed at enhancing reasoning capabilities and enabling autonomous internet navigation for "deep research"[8].

- **EU AI Act Published**: The European Union published the final language of its comprehensive AI Act, setting initial obligations to take effect on February 2, 2025[3].

- **AI Impact on Creativity**: Research suggests AI can boost individual creativity but may reduce overall diversity in creative output[7].

- **OpenAI Safety Concerns**: Reports emerged of internal concerns at OpenAI regarding the company's approach to AI safety testing and potential prioritization of commercial interests[9].

## Industry Updates

- **Writer Enhances AI Chat Apps**: Writer announced updates to its AI chat applications, introducing advanced graph-based retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and new transparency tools[1].

- **Meta's MobileLLM**: Meta AI developed MobileLLM, an efficient language model optimized for smartphones and resource-constrained devices[1].

- **Amazon's AI Hiring**: Amazon hired several co-founders and employees from AI startup Adept to boost its AI capabilities[1].

- **Baidu's AI Advancements**: Baidu launched an upgraded version of its AI model, Ernie 4.0 Turbo, and announced its AI user base has reached 300 million[1].

## AI in Business and Society

- **AI Energy Demands**: Tech giants like Microsoft, Google, and Amazon face challenges meeting climate goals due to rising energy demands from AI development[4].

- **AI in Agriculture**: Researchers see great potential for AI to help overcome agriculture's labor shortage[5].

- **AI in Healthcare**: The Canadian government invested $15 million in AI healthcare development through a Vancouver tech cluster program[4].

- **AI in Workplace**: A Thomson Reuters report predicts AI could save knowledge workers up to 12 hours per week by 2029[12].

## Regulatory and Ethical Considerations

- **Microsoft Exits OpenAI Board**: Microsoft withdrew its observer seat on the OpenAI board due to regulatory concerns[1].

- **EU Scrutiny of AI Partnerships**: EU antitrust regulators are examining partnerships between Microsoft and OpenAI, as well as Google and Samsung[1].

- **Corporate AI Readiness**: A survey by Berkeley Research Group found only about one-third of corporate leaders believe current and future AI regulations will provide necessary guardrails[12].
