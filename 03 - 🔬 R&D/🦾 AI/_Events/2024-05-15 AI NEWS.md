## Senate Unveils AI Roadmap
- A bipartisan group of senators, led by Senate Majority Leader Charles E. Schumer, released a 31-page blueprint for AI regulation and innovation[1][2][3]. 
- The roadmap proposes allocating at least $32 billion annually for non-defense AI research and development[2].
- It also outlines proposals for individual congressional committees to establish guardrails for AI to mitigate potential risks like discrimination, job displacement, and election interference[1][3].

## State-Level AI Regulation Efforts
- Colorado and Connecticut have been leading the charge on regulating AI at the state level[6].
- Connecticut's ambitious AI legislation faced a setback when the governor threatened a veto, citing concerns about stifling industry growth[6].
- In Colorado, the tech lobby is pressuring the governor to abandon the state's AI regulation bill[6].

## Concerns About AI's Impact on 2024 Election
- A national survey by the Elon Poll found that 78% of American adults expect abuses of AI systems will affect the outcome of the 2024 presidential election[4].
- The survey also found that a majority of respondents believe AI will be used to manipulate social media, generate fake information, and convince people not to vote[4].

## Criticism and Next Steps
- Some consumer advocacy groups have criticized the Senate's AI roadmap for not providing sufficient clarity on how it will protect individuals from AI's potential harms[1][3].
- Despite the setbacks in Connecticut and Colorado, lawmakers plan to reintroduce AI regulation bills next year and expect more states to propose similar legislation[6].
