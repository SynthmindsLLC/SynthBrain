## Government Initiatives and Investments in AI
- Senate Majority Leader Charles E. Schumer and the director of the U.S. National Science Foundation, Sethuraman Panchanathan, visited the University at Buffalo to discuss AI advancements and potential federal grants for the region. Schumer emphasized the importance of AI as a job creator and the need for responsible development to prevent negative consequences like deep fakes and election interference[1].
- President Biden directed the NSF to establish a National AI Research Resource to democratize access to AI resources for researchers, students, and companies. The initiative includes partnerships with various federal agencies and tech companies[1].
- The Empire AI Consortium was announced by New York Governor Kathy Hochul, with $400 million in funding for an advanced computing center to be shared by leading research universities in the state[1].

## Regulatory Actions on AI
- The SEC is cracking down on "AI washing," where companies falsely claim to use AI to attract investors or boost stock prices. The SEC settled charges with two investment advisors, Delphia and Global Predictions, for making false claims about their use of AI, resulting in fines totaling $400,000[2].

## Environmental Impact of AI
- A study found that AI systems for writing and illustrating emit significantly less carbon than humans performing the same tasks. However, the study's authors advocate for a collaborative approach between AI and human efforts rather than replacement[3].

## Corporate Acquisitions
- Yahoo acquired Artifact, an AI-driven personalized news platform founded by the co-founders of Instagram, Kevin Systrom and Mike Krieger. This move is part of Yahoo's strategy to strengthen its position in the digital and AI sectors[4].

## AI in the Job Market
- Indeed introduced an AI tool called 'Smart Sourcing' to automate recruiting processes, claiming to save employers time and potentially reduce hiring bias. The tool has been adopted by over 300 million job candidates on Indeed's platform[5].

## Creative Rights and AI
- Over 200 artists, including Stevie Wonder, Miranda Lambert, and Billie Eilish, submitted a letter through the Artist Rights Alliance, urging AI tech companies to respect artists' rights and not to use AI to infringe upon or devalue their work[6].

## International Relations and AI
- U.S. President Joe Biden and Chinese President Xi Jinping discussed Taiwan and AI in their first conversation since November. The discussion included concerns about China's "unfair trade policies and non-market economic practices" and the importance of preventing advanced U.S. technologies from being used to undermine security[7].
