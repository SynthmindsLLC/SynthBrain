Here's a summary of key AI news from July 19, 2024:

## Industry Developments

- GE HealthCare announced an agreement to acquire Intelligent Ultrasound Group's clinical AI software business for approximately $51 million. This acquisition aims to integrate AI-driven image analysis tools across GE's ultrasound portfolio to improve workflows and enhance ease-of-use for clinicians[11].

- Anthropic and Menlo Ventures launched a new $100 million AI fund to invest in AI startups. This comes as funding for AI startups more than doubled in Q2 2024 compared to Q1, topping $24 billion[14].

- C3 AI partnered with Google to launch GenAI for Government Programs, aiming to empower the public to find answers through an intuitive search and chat interface[14].

- CARTO introduced new Analytic AI Agents in private preview, leveraging large language models to provide a more intuitive interface for analyzing spatial data[14].

## Research and Innovation

- A study led by Kate Glazko at the University of Washington found that ChatGPT routinely ranked job seekers lower if their CVs mentioned awards implying disabilities like autism or blindness[16].

- Eric Slyman at Oregon State University developed computing instructions to train AI to be less biased against marginalized groups when generating image search results[16].

## Policy and Regulation

- The Cyberspace Administration of China is working to keep generative AI from straying into sedition, potentially slowing AI progress in China[9].

## Business and Economy

- An EY report indicates that executives are doubling down on AI investments, with those making larger investments already seeing impact[14].

- Exa raised $22 million in Series A funding for its technology that uses embedding models to convert web pages into embeddings, enhancing search processes[14].

## Education and Workforce

- NVIDIA offered new self-paced career development courses in AI and data science, including free access to introductory courses on topics like generative AI and CUDA[6].

- Robert Half urged employers to look beyond technical skills when hiring for AI talent, noting that not all roles require deep technical skills to deploy and use AI effectively[8].

These developments highlight the continued rapid advancement and integration of AI across various sectors, as well as growing attention to ethical considerations and potential biases in AI systems.
