This week saw significant AI news and developments across the tech industry, regulatory landscape, and even the G7 summit. Here are some of the top stories:

## Tech Industry AI Developments
- **Apple unveiled "Apple Intelligence"**, a new AI system aiming to enhance Siri's capabilities and offer more personalized features while prioritizing user privacy. This was announced alongside an OpenAI partnership at Apple's annual developers conference.[1][13]
- **Meta announced plans to use publicly shared European social media content to train its generative AI models**, but faced regulatory pushback. The Irish Data Protection Commission requested Meta delay this, putting the launch of its AI assistant in Europe on hold for now.[2][13]
- **Microsoft's Brad Smith met with Pope Francis** to discuss AI ethics. Microsoft sees the Vatican as a key partner in building global trust in its AI ambitions.[11]

## Regulatory and Political Landscape
- **U.S. Rep. Troy Carter voiced support for a federal body to monitor AI developments**, akin to the model used for cybersecurity.[6]  
- **The EU AI Act and other global regulatory efforts** like Japan's Hiroshima AI process are progressing, aiming to establish guidelines and oversight for generative AI.[10]
- **Colorado introduced new legislation to regulate AI and data privacy**, including disclosure requirements for AI-generated content and enhanced protections for minors' data.[7]

## Pope Francis' Address to G7 Leaders on AI
- In a historic first, **Pope Francis addressed the G7 summit**, delivering a speech on the ethical implications of artificial intelligence.[8][10][12][13]
- The Pope warned that AI could dehumanize relationships and called for "urgent" political action to guide its development in a way that protects human dignity.[8][10][11][13]
- He advocated for human oversight of AI decision-making, especially regarding weapons, and a ban on lethal autonomous weapons.[8][10][11]

## Other Notable Stories
- A study warned the **supply of public data to train AI language models could be depleted by 2032**, potentially hindering progress.[7]
- Bill Gates discussed his **$1B investment in a new nuclear power plant**, which he sees as key to powering the growing electricity demands of AI data centers.[9]

The G7 leaders are clearly taking the governance of AI seriously, with the Pope's speech adding moral weight to the discussions. As generative AI continues its rapid advancement, finding the right balance of innovation and oversight will be critical.
