## OpenAI Restricting Access in China and Other Regions

OpenAI, the company behind ChatGPT, has started taking steps to block API access to its AI tools and services in China and other unsupported regions, according to emails sent to developers. In response, Chinese AI firms like Baidu and Alibaba are moving swiftly to attract OpenAI users to their own platforms. OpenAI stated they are enforcing an existing policy to restrict access in regions where they do not offer support. This move comes amidst pressure from the U.S. government on tech companies to limit China's access to AI products.

## Record Labels Sue AI Music Generation Startups 

Major record companies Sony, Universal, and Warner have filed lawsuits against AI music generation startups Udio and Suno, alleging copyright infringement. The lawsuits claim these companies trained their AI models on copyrighted music without authorization. This case highlights the complex intellectual property issues arising as AI is used to create content in creative industries.

## Investors Remain Bullish on AI Despite Nvidia Stock Dip

Although shares of chipmaker Nvidia, a major player in the AI industry, recently tumbled, fueling some concerns of an AI bubble, many investors and asset managers expressed continued optimism about the transformative potential of artificial intelligence at the Bloomberg Invest summit. While exercising some caution, most see the heightened interest and discussion around AI as a positive indicator.

## Stability AI Seeks Revival with New CEO and Investors

Stability AI, the embattled startup behind the AI image generator Stable Diffusion, announced a significant new investment round led by Sean Parker, who will become the chairman of the board. The company also appointed a new CEO, Prem Akkaraju, formerly of Weta Digital, as it attempts to overcome legal challenges and controversy to capitalize on the popularity of generative AI.

## Fed Governor Downplays AI Threat to Central Banking Jobs

Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook stated that AI is unlikely to replace central bankers anytime soon, citing the nuance and precision required in their communications, where "every word is contested." However, she acknowledged the Fed is monitoring the potential economic impacts of AI closely.
