Here's a summary of the key AI news and developments for July 22, 2024:

## Major Industry Developments

- Nvidia is working on a version of its new flagship AI chip, the B200, for the Chinese market that would comply with U.S. export controls. The chip, tentatively named "B20", will be developed in partnership with Chinese distributor Inspur[1].

- OpenAI unveiled GPT-4o mini, a smaller and faster AI model meant to replace GPT-3.5 Turbo. The company claims it outperforms leading small AI models on reasoning tasks involving text and vision[9].

- OpenAI announced a partnership with Los Alamos National Laboratory to study how AI can advance research in healthcare and bioscience[9].

## Government and Regulation

- The U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) announced a funding opportunity for a new AI-focused Manufacturing USA institute. NIST anticipates funding up to $70 million over five years to establish and operate the institute[8].

- OpenAI changed its policy to allow military applications of its AI tools, removing language that previously prohibited use for "military and warfare" purposes[9].

- States in the U.S. are increasingly passing their own AI regulations in the absence of federal legislation. So far in 2024, 11 new states have enacted laws about AI use and regulation[11].

## Business and Economy

- A McKinsey report suggests generative AI could add up to $4.4 trillion in economic value across various use cases and industries[12].

- Amazon called for the development of global rules for responsible AI, emphasizing the need for international alignment on AI measures to protect economic prosperity and security[14].

## Research and Innovation

- Dublin City Council partnered with OpenAI to use GPT-4 for crafting personalized travel itineraries to support tourism[9].

- OpenAI is forming a Collective Alignment team to create a system for collecting and encoding public input on its models' behaviors[9].

## Societal Impact

- The rise of AI has renewed discussions about universal basic income. Sam Altman, CEO of OpenAI, funded a large-scale study on the impacts of no-strings-attached cash aid as a potential response to AI-driven job displacement[16].

- AI chatbots struggled to keep up with rapidly changing political news, highlighting limitations in their ability to process and report on real-time events[7].

These developments showcase the continued rapid advancement of AI technology, its growing impact across various sectors, and the ongoing efforts to address its societal implications and regulatory challenges.
