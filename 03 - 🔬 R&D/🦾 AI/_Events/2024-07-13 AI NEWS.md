## Key AI Developments

- **Amazon Launches AI Shopping Assistant**: Amazon introduced Rufus, an AI chatbot to help U.S. customers make more informed shopping decisions on its platform. Rufus uses generative AI to analyze product details and customer reviews[1].

- **DreamFlare AI's New Entertainment Platform**: San Francisco-based DreamFlare AI launched a platform offering AI-generated immersive stories ("Flips") and interactive short movies ("Spins")[1].

- **AI for Drunk Driver Detection**: Australian researchers developed an AI algorithm that can detect drunk drivers using in-vehicle cameras to analyze facial cues[1].

- **AI Predicts Rogue Waves**: University of Maryland researchers created an AI tool that can predict dangerous "killer" waves one minute in advance with 75% accuracy[1].

- **Tinder's AI Profile Picture Tool**: The dating app Tinder launched an AI tool to help users select their best profile pictures[1].

## AI in Surveillance and Security

- **Paris Olympics AI Surveillance**: France is deploying extensive AI-powered surveillance systems for the 2024 Paris Olympics, raising privacy concerns. The systems will monitor crowd movements, detect abandoned objects, and identify potential security threats[5].

## AI Startups and Funding

- Several U.S.-based AI startups raised significant funding in July 2024:
  - Hebbia: $130 million for AI-powered document search
  - Skild AI: $300 million for robotics technology
  - Bright Machines: $106 million for smart robotics and AI-driven software
  - Etched.ai: $120 million for AI chip development
  - EvolutionaryScale: $142 million for biological AI models[3]

## AI and Creative Industries

- Young artists are expressing concerns about the impact of generative AI on creative professions. A podcast series titled "The Creative Code" explores the contentious relationship between human creatives and AI[8].
