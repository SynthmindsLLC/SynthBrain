## OpenAI Discloses Misuse of Its AI Tools by Propagandists

OpenAI released a report disclosing that bad actors, including from Russia, China, Iran and Israel, had misused their AI tools to run influence operations. The propagandists used OpenAI's tools to generate social media comments, turn news articles into posts, and more, in an attempt to increase the scale and quality of their efforts. OpenAI detected and shut down the accounts to mitigate impact. This transparency report sets an important precedent for AI companies to share data on misuse with researchers.[2]

## Apple to Announce Major AI Initiative at WWDC 

At its upcoming Worldwide Developers Conference, Apple is expected to announce a partnership with OpenAI and introduce generative AI tools for its mobile OS. Integration of AI could boost iPhone sales and services. Key announcements may include enhancing Siri by turning it into an AI chatbot, addressing AI's impact on privacy/security, and AI-powered updates to the Vision Pro headset.[3]

## War for AI Talent Heats Up as Researchers Leave OpenAI

OpenAI has seen an exodus of about a dozen top AI researchers this year, including co-founder Ilya Sutskever. This reflects a broader industry trend, with an estimated 20,000 companies now recruiting AI experts after the launch of ChatGPT. The AI talent pool is becoming more dispersed as demand for machine learning skills grows.[4]

## U.S. Opens Antitrust Probes into Microsoft and Nvidia's AI Moves

The FTC will investigate Microsoft's $13B partnership with OpenAI and dominance in AI, while the DOJ will probe Nvidia's power in the market for AI chips. The agencies are concerned about the companies' control over key inputs to AI systems. The probes reflect growing regulatory scrutiny of the AI industry.[5]

## Debate Over AI's Role in Radiology Continues

Radiologists remain cautious about fully automatic AI systems that could operate without human oversight, despite a shortage of radiologists causing backlogs. AI executives argue the technology is ready and could reduce overtreating patients and disagreement between doctors. About 20% of breast cancers are missed in mammograms. AI also has potential for major cost savings.[6]
