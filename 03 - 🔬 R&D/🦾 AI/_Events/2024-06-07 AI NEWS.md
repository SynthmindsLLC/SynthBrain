## Apple's AI Strategy to be Unveiled at WWDC 2024

Apple is set to detail its generative AI strategy, dubbed "Apple Intelligence", at its Worldwide Developer Conference (WWDC) next week. While the full scope is unknown, expect to see AI-powered features in iOS 18 like notification summaries, auto-replies, an emoji creator, and photo editing. Apple will balance on-device AI with cloud capabilities through partnerships like OpenAI, while emphasizing privacy. [1][2]

## Meta's AI Data Scraping Raises Privacy Concerns 

Meta announced plans to use publicly available Instagram and Facebook posts in Europe to train its AI, sparking privacy worries. Meta already does this in the U.S. Privacy advocates are concerned about the lack of transparency around data usage, though Meta says it's following regulations. [5]

## Measuring AI's Impact on Jobs Remains Challenging

While the monthly U.S. jobs report provides insights into employment trends, it doesn't directly track jobs gained or lost due to AI. However, economists look for clues in areas like tech job categories and unemployment rates for recent grads in computational fields that could be impacted by AI automation. Companies are also increasingly citing "technological updates" vs. AI specifically when announcing layoffs. [3]

## Broadcom Gaining Attention as an AI Chipmaker 

Although Nvidia remains a Wall Street darling, analysts are increasingly bullish on Broadcom's potential in the AI chip market. Broadcom's wireless expertise, VMware acquisition, and diverse portfolio position it well to benefit from AI growth. [4]

## Key Takeaways

- Apple's measured approach to AI aims to balance innovation with privacy 
- Social media data usage for AI training is raising privacy concerns
- Quantifying AI's job impact remains difficult with current labor data
- The AI chipmaker competitive landscape is heating up beyond Nvidia
