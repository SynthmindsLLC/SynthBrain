Several significant AI-related news stories broke on April 30, 2024:

- The U.S. Department of Commerce announced that NIST has released four draft publications aimed at improving the safety and security of AI technologies. This includes an AI risk management framework, secure software development practices for generative AI, guidance on reducing risks from synthetic content, and a plan for global engagement on AI standards. The USPTO also published a request for comment on how AI could impact patent law.[1]

- A survey by the Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas found that about 25% of businesses in Texas expect AI will decrease staffing requirements or alter their workforce composition. 11% said AI has already reduced their reliance on human labor or will do so in the future.[2]

- Microsoft announced a $1.7 billion investment over the next four years to expand cloud services and AI in Indonesia. This includes building data centers, AI skilling initiatives for 2.5 million people across Southeast Asia by 2025, and support for Indonesia's growing developer community. Microsoft is collaborating with the Indonesian government on responsible AI development.[3][5] 

- Eight major U.S. newspapers owned by Alden Global Capital filed a lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft, accusing them of copyright infringement for using news articles to train AI models without permission. The publishers argue this hurts their subscription revenue and licensing opportunities. The case adds to the debate over data use in generative AI.[4]

- New York Governor Kathy Hochul announced the Empire AI consortium of seven institutions to promote responsible AI research and development, create jobs, and drive economic growth in the state.[7]
