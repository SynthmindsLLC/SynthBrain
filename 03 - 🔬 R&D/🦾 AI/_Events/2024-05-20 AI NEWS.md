- **Microsoft Introduces Copilot+ PCs with Advanced AI Features**: Microsoft has unveiled a new category of Windows PCs designed for AI, called Copilot+ PCs. These devices, starting at $999, feature powerful silicon capable of 40+ TOPS, all-day battery life, and access to advanced AI models. The first wave of Copilot+ PCs will be available from Microsoft Surface and OEM partners starting June 18.[3][6]

- **OpenAI Faces Controversy Over ChatGPT Voice Resembling Scarlett Johansson**: OpenAI has pulled an AI voice assistant that sounded strikingly similar to actress Scarlett Johansson, who voiced an AI system in the movie "Her." Johansson's lawyers are demanding answers from OpenAI about how the voice was developed, as the actress claims she declined requests to license her voice for the virtual assistant.[7][8]

- **The Opportunities and Risks of AI Acquisitions**: As AI continues to advance, companies are exploring the potential of AI acquisitions. While these acquisitions present significant opportunities, they also come with risks that need to be carefully navigated, such as compliance with labor laws and intellectual property concerns.[2]

- **The Potential Impact of AI on the American Workforce**: Labor economist David Autor discusses how AI could help American workers, despite concerns about job displacement. While historically, technological advances have hurt the incomes of middle- and working-class Americans, Autor believes that AI could create new jobs and shift the nature of expertise and its value.[1]

- **The Looming Battle Between Washington and Silicon Valley Over AI**: As the AI revolution unfolds, a political fight is brewing between Washington and Silicon Valley over who will benefit most from the technology and what, if anything, Washington should do to shape the industry's development. Regulators are warning that there is no AI exemption to existing laws, and big tech companies will face increased scrutiny, particularly on antitrust issues.[4]
