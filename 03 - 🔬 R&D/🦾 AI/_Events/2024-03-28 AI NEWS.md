Recent developments in AI as of March 28, 2024, cover a range of topics from government policy to energy concerns and the potential biases within AI systems. Here's a summary of the key news:

- **California AI Consumer Protections**
  The San Francisco Business Times published an opinion piece highlighting the need for consumer protections in California against the backdrop of Wall Street banks investing billions in AI research and patenting tools. The article emphasizes the urgency for regulations and public safeguards to keep pace with technological advancements[8].

- **AI's Energy Consumption**
  A report suggests that AI could be facing an "Energy Crisis" as the electricity required for training models like GPT-6 becomes a significant challenge. This raises concerns about the sustainability of AI's energy consumption[7].

- **AI in Brewing Beer**
  Researchers in Belgium are exploring the use of AI in brewing beer, investigating whether it can outperform human brewers in optimizing the chemical processes involved[6].

- **AI and Political Bias**
  The New York Times offers an interactive opinion piece that discusses the political biases of AI chatbots like ChatGPT. It includes a quiz to compare personal politics against the tendencies of AI models, which have been found to lean left of center and libertarian. The article calls for legislation to ensure transparency in AI training to prevent these biases from influencing users[5].

- **AI Tools for Home Use**
  Boston Magazine lists 15 AI tools that can be experimented with at home, ranging from drafting emails to mowing the lawn. The article also discusses the implications of AI in daily life and the potential for AI to assist with financial planning and other tasks[4].

- **Biden Administration's AI Policies**
  The Biden administration has announced new policies for the federal government's use of AI. These policies aim to ensure AI does not endanger Americans' rights and safety, improve transparency, and require federal agencies to designate a chief AI officer. Vice President Kamala Harris stated that these policies are intended to serve as a global model[3][2][1].

- **OMB AI Policy Announcement**
  The White House Office of Management and Budget (OMB) has issued its first policy on AI, following President Biden's executive order. This policy is part of a broader effort to ensure responsible and trustworthy use of AI in federal agencies, with a focus on governance, innovation, and risk management[1].

These articles provide a comprehensive view of the current state of AI, highlighting the importance of responsible development, ethical considerations, and the potential impact of AI on various aspects of society.
