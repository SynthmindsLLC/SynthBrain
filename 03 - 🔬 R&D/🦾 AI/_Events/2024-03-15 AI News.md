## 2024-03-15 News in AI

### FTC Inquiry into Reddit's AI Data-Licensing Practices

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) has initiated an inquiry into Reddit's practices regarding the licensing of user data to AI companies. This move comes as Reddit prepares for its Initial Public Offering (IPO). The inquiry focuses on Reddit's sale, licensing, and sharing of user-generated content with third parties for training artificial intelligence models. Reddit has expressed that it is not surprised by the FTC's interest due to the novel nature of these technologies and commercial arrangements. The company believes it has not engaged in any unfair or deceptive trade practices[1][6][8].

### TransUnion Launches OneTru

TransUnion, a major credit bureau, has introduced OneTru, an AI-powered data analytics platform. OneTru is designed to provide banks and fintechs with AI and machine learning tools for credit, anti-fraud, and marketing needs. The platform consists of four key layers: Data Management, Identity, Analytics, and Delivery, ensuring regulatory compliance. This initiative aims to offer a more complete and persistent picture of consumers[3].

### Other Notable AI Developments

- The EU has passed the AI Act, marking a significant step in AI regulation[2].
- Google has restricted chatbot responses to election queries, reflecting ongoing concerns about AI's role in disseminating information[2].
- Apple has acquired the DarwinAI startup, indicating a push towards enhancing AI capabilities in its products[5].

These developments highlight the growing intersection of AI technology with regulatory, ethical, and business considerations. The FTC's inquiry into Reddit's data-licensing practices underscores the increasing scrutiny of AI's impact on privacy and data usage.
