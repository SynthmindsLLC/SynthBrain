Mo Gawdat is an Egyptian entrepreneur, author, and former Chief Business Officer at Google X, known for his work in technology and innovation. He has authored international bestsellers such as "Solve for Happy" and "Scary Smart," and is the host of the podcast "Slo Mo: A Podcast with Mo Gawdat." His career spans over 30 years in tech, including roles at IBM Egypt, NCR Abu Dhabi, and Microsoft, before joining Google in 2007. Gawdat's work at Google X involved overseeing projects like self-driving cars and Google Life Sciences. After the tragic loss of his son Ali in 2014, Gawdat focused on researching happiness, leading to his book "Solve for Happy" and the founding of the initiative One Billion Happy. He is also a speaker who has given talks at various organizations and events[1][4][7].

Gawdat has been vocal about the potential dangers and ethical considerations of AI development. He predicts that AI will surpass human intelligence and emphasizes the urgency of preparing for this future. His insights into AI's impact on society and the need for a harmonized approach to education in the AI era are significant contributions to the discourse on technology and human values[3][5][14][15].

In addition to his writing and speaking engagements, Gawdat is involved in various projects aimed at reinventing consumerism and fostering happiness in the workplace. He has co-founded over 20 businesses and mentors numerous startups[6][7][8].

## Mo Gawdat's Background in Technology

Mo Gawdat is a prominent figure in the technology and innovation sector, with a career spanning over three decades. His journey in the tech world began with an engineering background, complemented by an MBA degree from Maastricht School of Management in the Netherlands[1][2]. Gawdat's early career took off at IBM Egypt as a systems engineer, where he first showcased his technical skills and problem-solving capabilities. His role at IBM involved working closely with technology solutions, setting the stage for his future endeavors in the tech industry[2].

After his stint at IBM, Gawdat moved to the United Arab Emirates, where he joined NCR Abu Dhabi to cover the non-finance sector. His career trajectory continued to ascend as he took on various roles at Microsoft over a span of seven-and-a-half years, further solidifying his expertise and leadership in the technology sector[2].

In 2007, Gawdat joined Google, marking a significant milestone in his career. He was instrumental in kickstarting Google's business in emerging markets, demonstrating a keen interest in leveraging technology to empower communities in these regions[1][7]. Over a period of 6 years, he initiated close to half of Google's operations worldwide, showcasing his exceptional ability to scale operations and drive innovation[7].

Gawdat's most notable role came in 2013 when he moved to Google's infamous innovation arm, Google X (now known simply as X). As the Chief Business Officer at Google X, he led the business strategy, planning, sales, business development, and partnerships. Google X is renowned for its radical approach to innovation, focusing on developing new technologies that aim for a tenfold improvement over existing solutions. Under Gawdat's leadership, the business team designed innovative business models and forged deep partnerships that enabled Google X to thrive. Projects such as Project Loon, Makani, and self-driving cars are some of the breakthrough innovations that emerged from Google X during his tenure[1][7].

Beyond his roles in established corporations, Gawdat is a serial entrepreneur who has co-founded more than 20 businesses in various sectors, including health and fitness, food and beverage, and real estate. He also serves as a board member in several technology, health and fitness, and consumer goods companies, as well as on several government technology and innovation boards in the Middle East and Eastern Europe[1][7].

Gawdat's contributions to the technology sector are not limited to his professional roles. He is deeply passionate about the implications of artificial intelligence (AI) and has authored books such as "Solve for Happy" and "Scary Smart," where he explores the intersection of technology, happiness, and the future of AI. His work emphasizes the importance of ethical considerations in AI development and the potential impact of technology on society[1][2][3][4].
