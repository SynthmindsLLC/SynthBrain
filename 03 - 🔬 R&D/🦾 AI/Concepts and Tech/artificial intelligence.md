Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a field of study in computer science that develops and studies intelligent machines. It involves the creation of systems that can perform tasks that would normally require human intelligence, such as understanding natural language, recognizing patterns, and making decisions. AI technology is widely used in various sectors including industry, government, and science[1].

AI encompasses several subfields:

1. **Machine Learning (ML)**: This is a field of AI that focuses on the development of statistical algorithms that can learn from and make predictions or decisions based on data. Machine learning algorithms are trained to find patterns and relationships in data, and they use historical data as input to make predictions, classify information, and even generate new content[2][12].

2. **Neural Networks**: These are a subset of machine learning and are at the heart of deep learning algorithms. They are inspired by the human brain, mimicking the way that biological neurons signal to one another. Neural networks rely on training data to learn and improve their accuracy over time[3][10].

3. **Natural Language Processing (NLP)**: This is a branch of AI that gives computers the ability to understand and respond to text or voice data. NLP combines computational linguistics with statistical, machine learning, and deep learning models, enabling computers to process human language and complete tasks such as translation, sentiment analysis, and topic segmentation[4].

4. **Robotics**: AI is also used in robotics to allow robots to handle tasks that are dangerous or inconvenient for humans. It also enables robots to learn from their experiences and adjust their actions based on new inputs[1].

AI Ethics is another important aspect of AI, which deals with the moral issues arising from the use and impact of AI. It includes considerations such as the fairness of AI decisions, the transparency and explainability of AI systems, the impact of AI on jobs and the economy, and the potential misuse of AI.
