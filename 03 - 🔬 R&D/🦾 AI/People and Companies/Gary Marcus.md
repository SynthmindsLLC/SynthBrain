## Background and Education
Gary Fred Marcus, born on February 8, 1970, is an American psychologist, cognitive scientist, and author, recognized for his work at the intersection of cognitive psychology, neuroscience, and artificial intelligence (AI)[4]. He developed an early interest in AI and began coding at a young age. Marcus completed his undergraduate studies in cognitive science at Hampshire College and pursued graduate education at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), where he was mentored by Steven Pinker and conducted research on language acquisition[4].

## Career and Contributions
Marcus is an Emeritus Professor of Psychology and Neural Science at New York University (NYU). He has made significant contributions to the understanding of human language development and cognitive neuroscience[1][2]. In 2014, he founded Geometric Intelligence, a machine learning company that was later acquired by Uber[4]. Marcus also co-founded Robust.AI, aiming to build a machine-learning platform for autonomous robots[4].

He has authored several books, including "The Algebraic Mind," "Kluge," "The Birth of the Mind," and the New York Times Bestseller "Guitar Zero." His most recent book, "Rebooting AI," co-authored with Ernest Davis, addresses the limitations of current AI technologies and proposes a path towards more reliable artificial intelligence[1][2][4].

## Critique of Contemporary AI
Marcus is a vocal critic of the hype surrounding AI, particularly deep learning neural networks. He has argued that these systems are overrated and lack the robustness necessary for real-world applications. He has engaged in public debates and discussions with other AI researchers, including Yann LeCun, and has called for a more reasoned approach to AI development that incorporates reasoning and logic[3].

In recent years, Marcus has focused on the potential dangers of AI, such as the spread of misinformation and security vulnerabilities. He has advocated for regulation and increased AI literacy among the public. His concerns extend to the misuse of copyrighted materials and the absence of common sense in AI systems[6].

## Media Presence and Advocacy
Marcus is a frequent contributor to media outlets like "The New Yorker," "Wired," and "The New York Times." He has become a go-to expert on AI in the media, participating in interviews and discussions on platforms such as NPR, CNN, BBC, and "60 Minutes"[3]. He also hosts a podcast titled "Humans vs. Machines" and runs a Substack newsletter where he shares his insights on AI[1][2].

## Criticism and Controversy
Despite his prominence, Marcus's expertise in AI has been questioned by some in the AI community. Critics argue that he lacks hands-on experience in AI development and has not contributed to the production of neural network technology or software used in the field[5]. However, his role as a skeptic and advocate for responsible AI development continues to influence discussions on the future of AI technology[5][6].
