Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) are a specialized type of neural network designed to work with graph data. They have been developed over the past decade to leverage the structure and properties of graphs, which are a natural way to express a set of objects and their connections[1].

GNNs are highly influenced by [[Convolutional Neural Networks]] (CNNs) and graph embedding. They are used in predicting nodes, edges, and graph-based tasks. Just like CNNs are used for image classification, GNNs are applied to graph structures to predict a class[2].

There are several types of GNNs, including [[Graph Convolutional Networks]] (GCNs), which are similar to traditional CNNs. GCNs learn features by inspecting neighboring nodes, aggregating node vectors, passing the result to the dense layer, and applying non-linearity using the activation function[2].

GNNs are capable of performing node-level, edge-level, and graph-level prediction tasks. They can handle graph data where each node is related to others by links of various types, a situation where traditional machine learning algorithms often fail[6].

GNNs have found applications in various fields. For instance, Amazon reported using GNNs for fraud detection and recommendation systems[7]. They have also made significant advancements in the AI research landscape, with applications in areas like Google Maps[4].

In summary, GNNs are a powerful tool in the field of deep learning, providing a way to work with complex graph data structures and solve prediction tasks that traditional neural networks may struggle with.

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have a wide range of applications across various domains due to their ability to process and analyze graph-structured data. Some of the notable applications include:

- **Drug Discovery**: GNNs can predict the potency of potential drugs by graphically modeling interactions between molecules[2].
- **Social Network Analysis**: They are used to analyze social networks, predicting information dissemination and community formation[2].
- **[[Computer Vision]]**: GNNs can analyze images and videos by modeling relationships between pixels or regions[2].
- **[[Natural Language Processing]] (NLP)**: They are applied in NLP for tasks such as understanding the structure and semantics of text[2].
- **Recommendation Systems**: GNNs help in suggesting products or content to users by understanding the complex relationships within user-item interaction data[3][7].
- **Fraud Detection**: Companies like Amazon use GNNs to detect fraudulent activities by analyzing transaction networks[7].
- **Bioinformatics**: In the field of genetics and bioinformatics, GNNs process graph structure data for various tasks[6].
- **Physics Simulation**: They are used for simulating physical systems that can be represented as graphs[3].
- **[[Knowledge Graphs]]**: GNNs help in knowledge representation and reasoning over knowledge graphs[3].
- **Transportation**: Google Maps uses GNNs to learn representations of transportation networks for improving route prediction and traffic management[7].

These applications demonstrate the versatility of GNNs in handling complex data structures and providing insights across different sectors.
